# Amyloidosis

## Overview

Amyloidosis is an umbrella term for diseases in which misfolded proteins form insoluble fibers—called amyloid fibrils—that build up in tissues and progressively damage organs. The two systemic forms receiving the most curative-therapy research are light-chain (AL) amyloidosis, caused by an abnormal clone of bone-marrow plasma cells making toxic antibody light chains, and transthyretin (ATTR) amyloidosis, caused by unstable transthyretin protein made mainly by the liver. ATTR may be inherited (variant ATTR) or age-associated without a disease-causing mutation (wild-type ATTR); the heart and peripheral nerves are particularly affected. [FDA: AL amyloidosis approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-darzalex-faspro-newly-diagnosed-light-chain-amyloidosis) [FDA: ATTR-CM overview](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-drug-heart-disorder-caused-transthyretin-mediated-amyloidosis)

Prognosis depends strongly on amyloid type, the organs involved, and how early the protein source is controlled; advanced heart involvement remains especially dangerous. Current care is therefore aimed at rapidly stopping production of the offending protein and managing organ failure. For newly diagnosed AL amyloidosis, the U.S. standard includes daratumumab plus bortezomib, cyclophosphamide, and dexamethasone (Dara-VCd/CyBorD); for ATTR cardiomyopathy, protein stabilizers such as tafamidis or acoramidis and the RNA-silencing drug vutrisiran can slow disease progression, alongside heart-failure and rhythm care. These treatments are important advances, but they do not reliably remove established deposits or constitute a cure. [FDA: Daratumumab approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-darzalex-faspro-newly-diagnosed-light-chain-amyloidosis) [FDA: Acoramidis approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-approvals-and-databases/drug-trials-snapshot-attruby) [HELIOS-B](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2409134)

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Research activity has accelerated markedly since 2020, particularly in ATTR and AL amyloidosis. The dominant questions are whether researchers can permanently switch off production of amyloid precursor proteins, eradicate the abnormal plasma-cell clone in AL, and actively clear deposits that already injure the heart, kidneys, and nerves. The field is approaching potentially disease-modifying combinations rather than a proven cure: precursor suppression is now clinically effective, but durable restoration of damaged organs and safe, validated deposit removal remain unresolved. [CRISPR-Cas9 in ATTR](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2107454) [NI006 antibody trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2303765) [NIH amyloidosis research update](https://officeofbudget.od.nih.gov/pdfs/FY25/br/FY%202025%20NIH%20Significant%20Items.pdf)

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

For ATTR, RNA-targeting therapies have demonstrated that deep liver production of transthyretin can improve meaningful clinical outcomes. In the phase 3 HELIOS-B trial, vutrisiran—an RNA interference medicine administered every three months—reduced the combined risk of death and recurrent cardiovascular events versus placebo in 655 people with ATTR cardiomyopathy, while also preserving walking distance and health-related quality of life. This is not a cure because the treatment must continue and does not directly eliminate deposited amyloid, but it establishes powerful precursor suppression as a foundation for future combination therapy. [HELIOS-B](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2409134)

The most explicitly curative concept in ATTR is one-time in vivo gene editing. NTLA-2001, also called nexiguran ziclumeran (nex-z), packages CRISPR-Cas9 messenger RNA and a guide RNA in lipid nanoparticles that preferentially enter liver cells and disrupt the *TTR* gene. The first human study showed substantial reductions in circulating transthyretin after one infusion, providing landmark proof that gene editing can be delivered directly into the body. Its phase 3 MAGNITUDE study in ATTR cardiomyopathy was later placed on FDA clinical hold on October 29, 2025 after a serious liver-safety event, underlining that permanent editing requires exceptionally strong long-term safety evidence. [CRISPR-Cas9 in ATTR](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2107454) [MAGNITUDE trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06128629?intr=NTLA-2001) [Intellia 2025 update](https://ir.intelliatx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/intellia-therapeutics-announces-fourth-quarter-and-full-year-8)

A complementary ATTR strategy is amyloid depletion: antibodies designed to bind the abnormal fibril structure and recruit immune cells to clear deposits. In a phase 1 study, Neurimmune’s NI006 was not associated with apparent drug-related serious adverse events; at higher doses, cardiac imaging measures of amyloid burden appeared to decline over 12 months. Coramitug (formerly PRX004) is another anti-ATTR fibril antibody; its phase 1 dose-escalation study in variant ATTR established early safety and pharmacologic evidence, but it was not designed to prove organ recovery or survival benefit. These programs matter because a true functional cure will probably require both stopping new protein production and removing existing deposits. [NI006 antibody trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2303765) [PRX004 phase 1 study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13506129.2024.2420809)

In AL amyloidosis, the most successful recent advance has been more effective destruction of the plasma-cell clone. In the phase 3 ANDROMEDA trial, adding subcutaneous daratumumab to CyBorD produced a hematologic complete-response rate of 42.1%, compared with 13.5% for CyBorD alone, and this regimen became the first FDA-approved treatment for newly diagnosed AL amyloidosis. Researchers are now testing whether BCMA-directed chimeric antigen receptor (CAR) T cells—patients’ own engineered immune cells—can produce much deeper, potentially durable eradication of the clone in relapsed or refractory disease. [FDA: Daratumumab approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-darzalex-faspro-newly-diagnosed-light-chain-amyloidosis) [NEXICART-2 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06097832)

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

NEXICART-2 is a recruiting phase 1b/2, single-arm trial sponsored by Nexcella of NXC-201, an autologous BCMA-targeted CAR-T therapy, in relapsed or refractory AL amyloidosis. The study’s primary goals are safety, dose selection, and hematologic and organ responses; an early 2025 ASCO report described seven treated participants, so efficacy conclusions remain preliminary. [NEXICART-2 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06097832) [NEXICART-2 ASCO abstract](https://ascopubs.org/doi/10.1200/JCO.2025.43.16_suppl.7508)

Amyloid-clearing antibody trials have produced both promise and important setbacks. AstraZeneca/Alexion’s phase 3 CARES trials of anselamimab (CAEL-101) plus standard plasma-cell therapy in advanced AL amyloidosis did not achieve statistical significance for their overall primary endpoints; later analyses reported a possible benefit in the kappa light-chain subgroup, which needs independent confirmation. Prothena’s phase 3 AFFIRM-AL trial of the fibril-binding antibody birtamimab also failed its primary mortality endpoint in May 2025, and development was discontinued. These negative results show that reducing or targeting deposits does not automatically translate into survival benefit. [CARES phase 3 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04512235) [AstraZeneca anselamimab update](https://news.cision.com/astrazeneca/r/update-on-anselamimab-in-al-amyloidosis%2Cc4207599) [AFFIRM-AL result](https://ir.prothena.com/investors/press-releases/news-details/2025/Prothena-Announces-Phase-3-AFFIRM-AL-Clinical-Trial-for-Birtamimab-in-Patients-with-AL-Amyloidosis-Did-Not-Meet-Primary-Endpoint/default.aspx)

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Researchers combine molecular and organ-level measurements. In AL studies, they measure serum free light chains and bone-marrow plasma cells to determine whether treatment has stopped the precursor source, then track organ responses through kidney protein loss, cardiac troponin, and NT-proBNP. In ATTR, studies measure circulating transthyretin after RNA therapy or gene editing and use genetic testing to distinguish variant from wild-type disease. [FDA: Daratumumab approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-darzalex-faspro-newly-diagnosed-light-chain-amyloidosis) [CRISPR-Cas9 in ATTR](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2107454)

To determine whether deposits are actually declining, investigators increasingly use echocardiography, technetium bone-tracer scintigraphy, and cardiac magnetic resonance imaging. Magnetic-resonance extracellular-volume mapping and scintigraphy can serve as quantitative, though still imperfect, surrogate measures of cardiac amyloid load; NI006 used both approaches in its early clinical trial. Laboratory protein studies, patient-derived samples, animal models, and cell-based systems are used to assess fibril binding, immune-mediated clearance, gene-editing specificity, and liver-targeted lipid-nanoparticle delivery before human testing. [Cardiac amyloidosis imaging](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36060088/) [NI006 antibody trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2303765)

## Leading Institutions and Funding

Major clinical and translational work is concentrated at specialized amyloidosis centers and multinational trial networks, including centers affiliated with Boston University, Mayo Clinic, University College London and the U.K. National Amyloidosis Centre, Memorial Sloan Kettering, Stanford, and City of Hope. Industry sponsors have supplied much of the late-stage development: Alnylam sponsors vutrisiran studies; Intellia and Regeneron developed the original CRISPR editing program; Neurimmune developed NI006; AstraZeneca/Alexion developed anselamimab; and Nexcella sponsors NXC-201. [HELIOS-B](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2409134) [CRISPR-Cas9 in ATTR](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2107454) [NEXICART-2 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06097832)

Public and nonprofit infrastructure is also important for this rare-disease field. NIH-supported programs span basic research on amyloid-forming proteins, diagnostic biomarkers and imaging, and experimental therapies; the Amyloidosis Research Consortium’s Amyloidosis Forum is a public-private FDA partnership intended to improve natural-history evidence and clinical-trial endpoints. The Amyloidosis Foundation provides annual research grants for early-career investigators and travel grants supporting participation in the International Amyloidosis Symposium. ## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The field’s major strength is that it now attacks amyloidosis at several levels: production of precursor protein, abnormal plasma cells, deposited fibrils, and organ dysfunction. RNA silencing and modern AL regimens have demonstrated that suppressing the disease source can improve clinical outcomes, while CRISPR editing offers a plausible single-administration approach for ATTR. Antibody programs have also established that amyloid deposits can potentially be targeted directly. [HELIOS-B](https://www.nejm.org/doi/10.1056/NEJMoa2409134) [FDA: Daratumumab approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-darzalex-faspro-newly-diagnosed-light-chain-amyloidosis) [NI006 antibody trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2303765)

The central limitation is that biochemical improvement is not equivalent to cure. Deposits may persist after precursor suppression, damaged hearts and nerves may not fully recover, and imaging changes may not reliably predict survival or function. Permanent gene editing raises concerns about liver toxicity, immune reactions, unintended edits, and irreversibility; CAR-T therapy can cause cytokine-release syndrome, infections, prolonged low blood counts, and requires complex individualized manufacturing. Failed phase 3 antibody trials in AL also show the difficulty of proving that fibril clearance improves outcomes in medically fragile patients. Access, diagnostic delay, trial eligibility restrictions, and the high cost of advanced biologics or cell therapies remain major practical barriers. ## Outlook and Future Directions

Amyloidosis is not yet close to a universal cure, because it comprises biologically distinct disorders and no current approach consistently combines permanent elimination of the source protein with safe removal of established deposits and durable organ recovery. The most important milestones to watch are long-term safety and efficacy outcomes for one-time ATTR gene editing, randomized evidence that anti-fibril antibodies improve survival and organ function, and whether BCMA CAR-T therapy can safely produce sustained deep remissions in AL amyloidosis. The most credible curative path is likely combination treatment: eliminate or durably silence the precursor source early, clear residual fibrils, and support damaged organs while they recover.
