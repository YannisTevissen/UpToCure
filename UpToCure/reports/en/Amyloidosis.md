# Amyloidosis

## Overview

Amyloidosis is a family of diseases, not one single illness: misfolded proteins form insoluble “amyloid” fibrils that deposit in organs and interfere with normal function [Amyloidosis overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/amyloidosis). The most common systemic types are immunoglobulin light-chain amyloidosis (AL), caused by abnormal antibody-producing plasma cells, and transthyretin amyloidosis (ATTR), caused by misfolded transthyretin protein that can damage the heart, nerves, or both [AHA cardiac amyloidosis statement](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000792). It mainly affects adults, and prognosis depends on the amyloid type, how early it is recognized, and whether the heart, kidneys, nerves, or gastrointestinal tract are involved [Amyloidosis overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/amyloidosis).

Current standard care is type-specific. In AL amyloidosis, treatment aims to stop production of toxic light chains using plasma-cell therapies such as bortezomib-based chemotherapy plus daratumumab, while supportive care manages heart failure, kidney disease, neuropathy, and other organ complications [NEJM ANDROMEDA daratumumab trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2028631). In ATTR amyloidosis, treatment aims to stabilize transthyretin or reduce its production; tafamidis established disease-modifying care for ATTR cardiomyopathy, and newer RNA-based drugs are extending the strategy [FDA tafamidis approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-tafamidis-meglumine-and-tafamidis-treatment-cardiomyopathy-caused-transthyretin) [NEJM HELIOS-B vutrisiran](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2409134). These treatments can slow or halt new amyloid formation, but most do not reliably remove established deposits, so early diagnosis remains central to prognosis [Amyloidosis Foundation treatment overview](https://amyloidosis.org/facts/treatments/).

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

From 2020 to 2026, amyloidosis research has become highly active because several platform technologies now target the root protein source rather than only treating organ failure [NEJM NTLA-2001 early trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2107454). The dominant questions are whether ATTR can be functionally cured by long-term transthyretin silencing or one-time TTR gene editing, whether AL amyloidosis can be driven into deep and durable plasma-cell remission, and whether antibodies can safely clear amyloid already deposited in organs [ClinicalTrials.gov MAGNITUDE NTLA-2001](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06128629) [CAEL-101 stage IIIa trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04512235) [AFFIRM-AL birtamimab trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04973137). The field is closest to a functional cure in ATTR, while AL amyloidosis still needs both faster control of the plasma-cell clone and proven organ-repair strategies [NEJM ANDROMEDA daratumumab trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2028631).

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

RNA therapeutics are a leading curative-direction strategy for ATTR amyloidosis because they lower the liver’s production of transthyretin, the precursor protein that forms amyloid [HELIOS-A vutrisiran](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(22)00833-9/fulltext). Patisiran, a small interfering RNA drug, showed benefit in ATTR cardiomyopathy in the APOLLO-B trial, including effects on functional capacity and patient-reported health status [APOLLO-B patisiran](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2300757). Vutrisiran, a longer-acting RNA interference drug given by subcutaneous injection, improved hereditary ATTR polyneuropathy outcomes in HELIOS-A and reduced all-cause mortality and recurrent cardiovascular events in ATTR cardiomyopathy in HELIOS-B [HELIOS-A vutrisiran](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lancet/article/PIIS0140-6736(22)00833-9/fulltext) [NEJM HELIOS-B vutrisiran](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2409134).

Gene editing is the boldest ATTR approach because it aims to permanently switch off TTR production after one treatment. NTLA-2001 uses lipid nanoparticles to deliver CRISPR-Cas9 gene-editing machinery to liver cells, and the first-in-human report showed dose-dependent reductions in serum transthyretin after a single infusion in people with hereditary ATTR amyloidosis with polyneuropathy [NEJM NTLA-2001 early trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2107454). The phase 3 MAGNITUDE trial is testing NTLA-2001 in ATTR cardiomyopathy, making long-term safety, durability, off-target editing risk, and clinical outcome benefit key milestones [ClinicalTrials.gov MAGNITUDE NTLA-2001](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06128629).

Transthyretin stabilizers remain important because they prevent the TTR tetramer from falling apart, which is an early step in amyloid formation. Tafamidis reduced mortality and cardiovascular hospitalizations in ATTR cardiomyopathy before the current period and became a standard disease-modifying therapy [ATTR-ACT tafamidis](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1805689). Acoramidis, a newer stabilizer designed to achieve strong transthyretin stabilization, improved a hierarchical clinical endpoint in symptomatic ATTR cardiomyopathy in ATTRibute-CM [NEJM acoramidis ATTRibute-CM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2305434).

Antisense oligonucleotides are another way to reduce transthyretin production. Eplontersen, a ligand-conjugated antisense drug, produced significant and clinically meaningful improvements in serum transthyretin concentration, neuropathy impairment, and quality of life in hereditary ATTR amyloidosis with polyneuropathy in NEURO-TTRansform [JAMA eplontersen NEURO-TTRansform](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2808958). Like RNA interference drugs, antisense therapy may be “functionally curative” only if sustained protein suppression prevents progression for many years and if organ damage is not already irreversible [AHA cardiac amyloidosis statement](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000792).

For AL amyloidosis, the major 2020–present breakthrough is adding anti-CD38 plasma-cell therapy to first-line treatment. The phase 3 ANDROMEDA trial showed that subcutaneous daratumumab plus cyclophosphamide, bortezomib, and dexamethasone produced higher hematologic complete response rates and better organ responses than chemotherapy alone [NEJM ANDROMEDA daratumumab trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2028631). This approach is not a universal cure, but rapid and deep reduction of amyloid-forming light chains is the essential first step toward organ recovery [Systemic AL amyloidosis review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41409-022-01666-3).

Direct amyloid-clearing antibodies are intended to solve the problem left after protein production is controlled: existing deposits in the heart, kidneys, and other organs. CAEL-101 is being tested in two phase 3 trials for Mayo stage IIIa and IIIb AL amyloidosis with cardiac involvement, with survival and safety outcomes designed for patients at high risk of early death [CAEL-101 stage IIIa trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04512235) [CAEL-101 stage IIIb trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04504825). Birtamimab did not significantly improve the primary endpoint in the overall VITAL phase 3 population, but a post hoc Mayo stage IV signal led to the confirmatory AFFIRM-AL phase 3 trial in very advanced cardiac AL amyloidosis [Blood VITAL birtamimab](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/141/10/1208/486305/Birtamimab-plus-standard-of-care-in-light-chain) [AFFIRM-AL birtamimab trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04973137).

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

The most important recent ATTR cardiomyopathy trials are late-stage and outcome-focused. HELIOS-B is a phase 3 randomized trial of vutrisiran in ATTR cardiomyopathy and reported reductions in all-cause mortality and recurrent cardiovascular events [NEJM HELIOS-B vutrisiran](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2409134). ATTRibute-CM is a phase 3 trial of acoramidis in ATTR cardiomyopathy and reported improvement on a hierarchical endpoint that included mortality, cardiovascular hospitalization, NT-proBNP, and six-minute walk distance [NEJM acoramidis ATTRibute-CM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2305434). MAGNITUDE is a phase 3 randomized trial of the in vivo CRISPR therapy NTLA-2001 in ATTR cardiomyopathy and represents the highest-upside attempt to replace chronic therapy with a one-time intervention [ClinicalTrials.gov MAGNITUDE NTLA-2001](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06128629).

In AL amyloidosis, ANDROMEDA has already changed first-line therapy by supporting daratumumab-CyBorD for newly diagnosed patients [NEJM ANDROMEDA daratumumab trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2028631). Current experimental approaches focus on adding amyloid-directed antibodies to standard plasma-cell therapy, especially in cardiac AL where early mortality remains high [CAEL-101 stage IIIa trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04512235) [AFFIRM-AL birtamimab trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04973137). Autologous stem-cell transplantation can produce durable remissions in selected AL patients, but it is limited by frailty, organ involvement, and treatment-related risk in advanced cardiac disease [Systemic AL amyloidosis review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41409-022-01666-3).

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Researchers measure cure-oriented progress with both molecular and organ-level tools. ATTR studies track serum transthyretin lowering, cardiac biomarkers such as NT-proBNP and troponin, six-minute walk distance, quality-of-life scores, echocardiography, cardiac magnetic resonance imaging, and nuclear scintigraphy to determine whether protein control translates into organ benefit [AHA cardiac amyloidosis statement](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000792). Delivery technology is central: RNA interference and CRISPR programs use liver-targeted delivery systems such as lipid nanoparticles, while ligand-conjugated antisense drugs are designed to improve uptake and dosing convenience [NEJM NTLA-2001 early trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2107454) [JAMA eplontersen NEURO-TTRansform](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jama/fullarticle/2808958).

AL amyloidosis studies use hematologic response, organ response, cardiac staging, and survival endpoints because stopping light-chain production and repairing organ damage are related but separate goals [NEJM ANDROMEDA daratumumab trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2028631). Antibody trials against amyloid deposits must prove that removing fibrils improves outcomes people feel, such as survival, hospitalization, exercise tolerance, kidney function, and daily functioning [Blood VITAL birtamimab](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/141/10/1208/486305/Birtamimab-plus-standard-of-care-in-light-chain).

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The strongest progress is in ATTR amyloidosis, where multiple treatments now target the causal protein and where RNA silencing has shown outcome benefit in cardiomyopathy [NEJM HELIOS-B vutrisiran](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2409134). The biggest scientific limitation is that reducing or stabilizing transthyretin may not fully reverse established cardiac amyloid, so patients diagnosed late may still live with heart failure despite excellent biochemical control [AHA cardiac amyloidosis statement](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000792). Gene editing could improve convenience and adherence, but it raises special questions about irreversible on-target editing, off-target effects, long-term monitoring, and equitable access [NEJM NTLA-2001 early trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2107454).

For AL amyloidosis, daratumumab-based therapy has improved the speed and depth of hematologic response, but early mortality in advanced cardiac disease remains a major unsolved problem [NEJM ANDROMEDA daratumumab trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2028631). Amyloid-clearing antibodies are scientifically attractive, but the field still needs decisive phase 3 evidence that they improve survival and function, not only biomarkers [Blood VITAL birtamimab](https://ashpublications.org/blood/article/141/10/1208/486305/Birtamimab-plus-standard-of-care-in-light-chain). Across amyloidosis types, persistent barriers include delayed diagnosis, correct typing of amyloid deposits, high drug costs, under-recognition in community practice, and uneven access to specialist centers [Amyloidosis Foundation treatment overview](https://amyloidosis.org/facts/treatments/) [UK National Amyloidosis Centre](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/amyloidosis/).

## Outlook and Future Directions

A single universal cure is unlikely because amyloidosis can arise from different precursor proteins, but type-specific functional cures are becoming plausible [Amyloidosis overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/liver-disease/amyloidosis). The milestones to watch are long-term safety and outcome data for NTLA-2001, real-world durability of vutrisiran and other TTR silencers in ATTR cardiomyopathy, adoption of next-generation stabilizers such as acoramidis, phase 3 survival results for CAEL-101 and birtamimab in cardiac AL amyloidosis, and earlier diagnosis before irreversible organ injury occurs [ClinicalTrials.gov MAGNITUDE NTLA-2001](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06128629) [NEJM HELIOS-B vutrisiran](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2409134) [NEJM acoramidis ATTRibute-CM](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2305434) [CAEL-101 stage IIIa trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04512235) [AFFIRM-AL birtamimab trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04973137). As of May 2026, ATTR amyloidosis appears closest to a functional cure through durable TTR suppression or editing, while AL amyloidosis will require both durable plasma-cell control and reliable removal or healing of damaged organs [Systemic AL amyloidosis review](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41409-022-01666-3).
