# Alkaptonuria

## Overview

Alkaptonuria (AKU) is an ultra-rare, autosomal-recessive metabolic disease caused by disease-causing changes in both copies of the *HGD* gene. The missing or impaired homogentisate 1,2-dioxygenase (HGD) enzyme allows homogentisic acid (HGA) to accumulate. Urine may darken after standing, but the most disabling effects usually emerge later: HGA-derived pigment deposits in connective tissue (“ochronosis”), leading to early, progressive arthritis of the spine and large joints, and sometimes heart-valve disease, kidney or prostate stones, and hypothyroidism. In the United States, estimated incidence is approximately 1 in 250,000 to 1,000,000 live births; symptoms of ochronosis generally arise after age 30 and arthritis often begins in the third decade. [GeneReviews overview](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK1454/)

There is still no approved treatment that repairs a patient’s faulty *HGD* gene or reliably reverses established joint destruction. Current care combines monitoring and treatment of complications—pain management, physical and occupational therapy, joint replacement when necessary, and cardiac, renal, thyroid, and urologic surveillance—with HGA-lowering treatment using nitisinone. In the United States, the FDA approved Harliku (nitisinone) on June 10, 2025, to reduce urinary HGA in adults with AKU; in Europe, Orfadin (nitisinone) is authorized for adults with AKU. [FDA orphan-drug approval record](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=942723) [EMA Orfadin information](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/orfadin)

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Since 2020, AKU research has been active but concentrated in a small number of international centers. The central near-term question has been how early and how effectively nitisinone can prevent HGA-driven damage, while the principal curative question is whether liver-directed replacement of *HGD* can safely restore metabolic function for the long term. The field has reached convincing human evidence for disease modification with nitisinone and compelling mouse proof-of-concept for gene replacement, but it has not yet reached a human gene-therapy trial or a clinical cure. [SONIA 2 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32822600/) [AKU Society gene-therapy program](https://akusociety.org/other-research/) [AAV-*HGD* mouse preprint](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3198744/)

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

**Small-molecule metabolic blockade: nitisinone.** Nitisinone inhibits 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate dioxygenase, an enzyme upstream of HGA production. In the pivotal SONIA 2 randomized trial, 138 adults aged 25 years or older were assigned to 10 mg daily nitisinone or no treatment for four years. At 12 months, urinary HGA fell by 99.7% relative to control, and the treated group had a significantly smaller increase in the clinical AKU severity score at 48 months. This is a major advance because it reduces the toxic metabolite driving disease, but it is not a genetic cure: patients remain dependent on ongoing therapy and already damaged cartilage is not regenerated. [SONIA 2 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32822600/)

Recent follow-up analyses have made the clinical effect more concrete. A United Kingdom National Alkaptonuria Centre study reported that 2 mg daily nitisinone was associated with decreased visible eye and ear ochronosis and a 19.1% decrease in pigment measured in repeat ear-cartilage biopsies. A radiographic SONIA 2 analysis found only a modest slowing of spinal progression over four years, with a statistically significant difference confined to the combined thoracic-spine score. Together, these findings support suppression—and some partial reversal—of pigment deposition, while also showing why early treatment is likely more valuable than attempting to rescue advanced skeletal disease. [Ochronosis reversal study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32904992/) [Spinal-radiology analysis](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3178320/)

**Gene replacement therapy.** The most important curative-direction development is liver-directed adeno-associated virus (AAV) gene therapy designed to deliver a functional human *HGD* gene to hepatocytes, the liver cells that normally carry out most HGA metabolism. A 2026 preprint from collaborators at the University of Liverpool, Vrije Universiteit Brussel, and partner institutions reported that an AAV2/8 vector carrying codon-optimized human *HGD* achieved liver-predominant transduction in *Hgd*-deficient mice, restored plasma HGA to near wild-type concentrations at 12 weeks, reduced urinary HGA and HGA-derived metabolites, and did so without the elevation of upstream tyrosine-pathway metabolites caused by nitisinone. These results are a genuine proof-of-concept for metabolic correction, but remain preclinical and unpublished in a peer-reviewed journal as of August 8, 2026. [AAV-*HGD* mouse preprint](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3198744/)

No AKU-specific gene-editing, RNA-silencing, messenger-RNA replacement, cell-therapy, or enzyme-replacement program has yet produced comparable published human efficacy data. The current experimental emphasis is therefore conventional in vivo gene addition—supplying a functional *HGD* copy—rather than editing each patient’s individual variants. The AKU Society and University of Liverpool describe their gene-therapy work as a preliminary PhD program intended to build a foundation for a future cure. [AKU Society gene-therapy program](https://akusociety.org/other-research/)

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

The landmark recent clinical study is SONIA 2 (NCT01916382), a phase 3, international, multicenter, open-label, evaluator-blinded randomized trial sponsored by the University of Liverpool and conducted in the United Kingdom, France, and Slovakia. It compared 10 mg daily nitisinone with no treatment in 138 adults for four years. The trial met its biochemical endpoint, produced a 99.7% reduction in urinary HGA at 12 months, and showed slower progression on the clinical AKU severity score at 48 months; it was funded through the European Commission’s Seventh Framework Programme. [SONIA 2 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32822600/) [DevelopAKUre project record](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/304985)

Post-hoc work from the same trial continues to test whether biochemical suppression translates into organ-specific benefit. A 2025 analysis of echocardiographic data reported a small, exploratory treatment-associated difference in the progression of peak aortic-valve pressure gradient, but the authors emphasized that the post-hoc design and small absolute effect mean the result is hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. Meanwhile, the AAV-*HGD* program is an animal study, not a registered human trial; no clinical gene-replacement trial for AKU was identified in the sources reviewed for this report. [Aortic-stenosis SONIA 2 analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41589138/) [AAV-*HGD* mouse preprint](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3198744/)

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

AKU researchers use complementary biochemical, clinical, imaging, and genetic approaches. Human studies measure 24-hour urinary HGA, blood HGA and tyrosine, clinical severity scores, range of motion, patient outcomes, eye and ear pigmentation, radiographs, echocardiography, and tissue biomarkers. SONIA 2 illustrates the practical strategy: use HGA reduction as a direct pharmacodynamic measure while following patients for years to detect changes in the slower-moving joint, spine, and cardiovascular manifestations. [SONIA 2 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32822600/) [Spinal-radiology analysis](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3178320/)

The curative research platform relies on *Hgd*-deficient mice, liver-specific AAV vectors, and molecular confirmation of gene delivery and enzyme expression using methods such as DNA in situ hybridization, reverse-transcription quantitative PCR, and protein analysis. Researchers pair targeted liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry with broader metabolomics to determine not only whether HGA falls, but whether normal pathway flux and wider metabolic balance are restored. [AAV-*HGD* mouse preprint](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3198744/)

## Leading Institutions and Funding

The University of Liverpool and its associated National Alkaptonuria Centre have led much of the clinical-development work, including SONIA 2 and long-term natural-history and imaging studies. Key international collaborators include Royal Liverpool University Hospital, Hôpital Necker–Enfants Malades in Paris, Slovakia’s National Institute of Rheumatic Diseases and Biomedical Research Center, the AKU Society, Nordic Bioscience, clinical-research partners, and Swedish Orphan Biovitrum (Sobi). [DevelopAKUre project record](https://cordis.europa.eu/project/id/304985)

The major enabling investment was DevelopAKUre, a European Commission FP7 project that ran from November 2012 through January 2019 but generated the evidence published during the 2020s. Its total project cost was €11.21 million, including a €6.00 million EU contribution, and it directly supported dose-finding, phase 3 evaluation, biomarker development, and pediatric natural-history work. More recently, Vrije Universiteit Brussel and University of Liverpool investigators have advanced the AAV-*HGD* gene-replacement program, while the AKU Society reports supporting an early-stage PhD initiative aimed at a future gene-therapy cure. ## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The strongest result in AKU research is the reproducible ability of nitisinone to nearly eliminate HGA production and slow overall clinical progression. It is orally administered, has randomized-trial evidence, and now has U.S. and European adult regulatory pathways. The most encouraging curative result is that AAV-mediated *HGD* replacement can substantially normalize HGA metabolism in a relevant mouse model without nitisinone-associated buildup of tyrosine-pathway metabolites. [SONIA 2 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32822600/) [FDA orphan-drug approval record](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=942723) [AAV-*HGD* mouse preprint](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3198744/)

However, nitisinone shifts metabolism toward high blood tyrosine, requiring dietary management and monitoring; corneal keratopathy and other tyrosine-related complications are important safety concerns. Its benefits on slowly progressive structural outcomes have been modest over four years, and it cannot replace cartilage or undo all established joint, spinal, or valve damage. For gene therapy, major unanswered questions include dose selection, immune responses to AAV, durability of liver expression, re-dosing feasibility, manufacturing, long-term safety, and whether metabolic correction begun after ochronosis can meaningfully improve existing disease. ## Outlook and Future Directions

As of August 8, 2026, AKU is closer to durable disease control than to a proven cure. The next milestones to watch are peer-reviewed confirmation and longer follow-up of AAV-*HGD* correction in mice, dose and toxicology studies in larger models, a formal clinical candidate and regulatory filing for a first-in-human gene-therapy trial, and evidence that early HGA suppression can prevent—not merely slow—ochronosis and irreversible organ damage. If liver-directed *HGD* replacement proves safe, durable, and scalable in people, it could be a one-time disease-correcting treatment; at present, that prospect remains promising but preclinical.
