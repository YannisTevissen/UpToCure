# Amyotrophic Lateral Sclerosis

## Overview
Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) is a progressive neurodegenerative disease in which motor neurons—the nerve cells that control voluntary movement—degenerate in the brain and spinal cord, leading to worsening weakness, speech and swallowing problems, respiratory failure, and usually death within a few years of symptom onset, although the pace varies widely [NINDS ALS overview](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-als). Most ALS is sporadic, meaning it occurs without a clear family history, while a minority is inherited through genes such as SOD1, C9orf72, FUS, and TARDBP [NINDS ALS overview](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-als).

There is still no broadly curative treatment for ALS, so current care combines disease-modifying medicines with multidisciplinary supportive care, noninvasive ventilation, nutrition support, communication aids, symptom control, and palliative care [NINDS ALS overview](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-als). Riluzole remains a long-used oral survival-extending therapy, edaravone is available in intravenous and oral forms for selected patients, and tofersen is approved for adults with ALS caused by SOD1 mutations [NINDS ALS overview](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-als), [FDA oral edaravone approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-oral-form-edaravone-als), [FDA tofersen approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-treatment-rare-genetic-form-als).

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)
Since 2020, ALS research has become more genetically targeted and trial-focused: the field is testing antisense oligonucleotides, which are short synthetic RNA-like drugs that reduce or alter production of disease-related proteins, along with small molecules, stem-cell products, platform trials, and biomarker-guided studies using neurofilament light chain, a blood or cerebrospinal-fluid marker of nerve-cell injury [NEJM tofersen trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2204705), [HEALEY platform trial design](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36107416/). The community is closer to precision treatment for defined genetic subtypes than to a universal cure, as shown by the SOD1-targeted approval of tofersen and the later withdrawal of sodium phenylbutyrate/taurursodiol after confirmatory failure [FDA tofersen approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-treatment-rare-genetic-form-als), [FDA Relyvrio withdrawal](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/amylyx-pharmaceuticals-announces-voluntary-withdrawal-relyvrio-sodium-phenylbutyrate-and-taurursodiol).

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies
The clearest recent therapeutic breakthrough is tofersen for SOD1-ALS, a rare inherited form of ALS. In the VALOR trial, tofersen did not meet its main clinical endpoint at 28 weeks, but it substantially lowered SOD1 protein and neurofilament light chain, and longer follow-up suggested slower decline when treatment began earlier [NEJM tofersen trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2204705). The FDA granted accelerated approval in 2023 because reduced neurofilament light chain was judged reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit in this severe genetic subtype [FDA tofersen approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-treatment-rare-genetic-form-als).

Tofersen also changed the prevention strategy for inherited ALS. The ATLAS study is testing whether starting tofersen before symptoms, in SOD1 mutation carriers who show elevated neurofilament light chain, can delay or prevent clinical disease [ATLAS tofersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04856982). This is not yet a cure, but it is one of the clearest recent moves toward intervening before irreversible motor-neuron loss [ATLAS tofersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04856982).

Other RNA-targeted therapies are extending the same logic to additional genetic ALS subtypes. Ulefnersen, also known as ION363, is being tested for FUS-associated ALS in a phase 3 trial sponsored by Ionis Pharmaceuticals, reflecting a strategy of lowering a toxic or misprocessed disease-related RNA/protein in a genetically defined group [FUS-ALS ulefnersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04768972). These approaches are especially promising for rare monogenic ALS, but they require spinal-fluid delivery, careful safety monitoring, and a separate development path for each gene target [FUS-ALS ulefnersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04768972), [NEJM tofersen trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2204705).

Small-molecule therapy has produced mixed lessons. Sodium phenylbutyrate/taurursodiol, marketed as Relyvrio in the United States, slowed functional decline in the phase 2 CENTAUR trial and received FDA approval in 2022 [NEJM CENTAUR trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1916945), [FDA Relyvrio approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-new-treatment-amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis). However, the sponsor voluntarily withdrew it from the market in 2024 after the larger PHOENIX phase 3 trial did not confirm benefit, underscoring how difficult it is to translate early ALS signals into durable clinical efficacy [FDA Relyvrio withdrawal](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/amylyx-pharmaceuticals-announces-voluntary-withdrawal-relyvrio-sodium-phenylbutyrate-and-taurursodiol).

Cell-based therapy remains experimental. A phase 3 trial of NurOwn, an autologous mesenchymal stromal-cell product engineered to secrete neurotrophic factors, did not meet its prespecified primary endpoint in the overall ALS population, although biomarker and subgroup analyses continued to generate debate about whether selected patients might benefit [NurOwn phase 3 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35583916/). At present, cell therapy has not delivered a reproducible cure or broadly accepted disease-modifying effect for ALS [NurOwn phase 3 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35583916/).

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches
The HEALEY ALS Platform Trial, led through the Sean M. Healey & AMG Center at Massachusetts General Hospital, is a major phase 2/3 platform trial designed to test multiple investigational regimens efficiently against shared infrastructure and common outcome measures [HEALEY platform trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04297683), [HEALEY platform trial design](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36107416/). Its importance is methodological as much as therapeutic: ALS trials have historically been slow and underpowered, and the platform model allows faster addition and removal of regimens while preserving randomization and regulatory-grade data collection [HEALEY platform trial design](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36107416/).

Notable recent and ongoing targeted studies include VALOR and its extension for tofersen in symptomatic SOD1-ALS, ATLAS for presymptomatic SOD1 mutation carriers, and the phase 3 ulefnersen trial for FUS-ALS [NEJM tofersen trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2204705), [ATLAS tofersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04856982), [FUS-ALS ulefnersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04768972). Recently completed symptomatic or broad-population programs include CENTAUR/PHOENIX for sodium phenylbutyrate/taurursodiol and the NurOwn phase 3 cell-therapy study, both of which illustrate the gap between encouraging early signals and confirmatory success [NEJM CENTAUR trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa1916945), [FDA Relyvrio withdrawal](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/amylyx-pharmaceuticals-announces-voluntary-withdrawal-relyvrio-sodium-phenylbutyrate-and-taurursodiol), [NurOwn phase 3 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35583916/).

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches
Recent ALS cure-oriented research increasingly stratifies patients by genotype, stage, and biomarker status rather than treating ALS as one uniform disease [NEJM tofersen trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2204705), [ATLAS tofersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04856982). Neurofilament light chain has become especially important because it can reflect motor-neuron injury, helped support accelerated approval of tofersen, and is being used to identify high-risk presymptomatic SOD1 carriers for preventive treatment [FDA tofersen approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-treatment-rare-genetic-form-als), [ATLAS tofersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04856982).

Delivery remains a central scientific problem. Antisense therapies such as tofersen and ulefnersen are delivered into the cerebrospinal fluid rather than as simple pills, while small molecules aim for easier systemic delivery and cell therapies require individualized manufacturing and administration [NEJM tofersen trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2204705), [FUS-ALS ulefnersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04768972), [NurOwn phase 3 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35583916/). Trial methodology is also changing, with master-protocol platform designs and shared outcome infrastructure intended to make ALS drug testing faster, less duplicative, and more statistically efficient [HEALEY platform trial design](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36107416/).

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges
The field’s strongest recent advance is proof that a genetically targeted ALS therapy can change a disease biomarker and obtain regulatory approval, creating a model for other monogenic subtypes [FDA tofersen approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-treatment-rare-genetic-form-als). The main limitation is that SOD1-ALS represents only a small fraction of ALS, and most patients still have sporadic or genetically complex disease without a validated disease-reversing treatment [NINDS ALS overview](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-als).

The hardest challenges are biological timing, heterogeneity, and measurement. Motor neurons may be lost before diagnosis, different genetic and sporadic forms may require different treatments, and functional scales can be noisy over short trials, which helps explain why early signals such as sodium phenylbutyrate/taurursodiol and NurOwn did not become confirmed broad cures [FDA Relyvrio withdrawal](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/amylyx-pharmaceuticals-announces-voluntary-withdrawal-relyvrio-sodium-phenylbutyrate-and-taurursodiol), [NurOwn phase 3 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35583916/). Access is also a practical barrier because genetic testing, lumbar-puncture delivery, biomarker monitoring, multidisciplinary ALS clinics, and trial participation are not equally available to all patients [NEJM tofersen trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2204705), [ATLAS tofersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04856982).

## Outlook and Future Directions
A universal ALS cure is not imminent, but the field is no longer limited to purely supportive care: the most realistic near-term milestones are preventive or very-early treatment for SOD1 carriers, confirmatory success for additional antisense programs such as FUS-ALS, better use of neurofilament and other biomarkers to shorten trials, and maturation of platform-trial infrastructure that can rapidly test combination and mechanism-based therapies [ATLAS tofersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04856982), [FUS-ALS ulefnersen trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04768972), [HEALEY platform trial design](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36107416/). The likely path to curing ALS is not one single drug for everyone, but a staged precision-medicine strategy: identify the driver biology early, intervene before irreversible motor-neuron loss, and combine targeted disease modification with high-quality supportive care [NINDS ALS overview](https://www.ninds.nih.gov/health-information/disorders/amyotrophic-lateral-sclerosis-als), [NEJM tofersen trial](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2204705).
