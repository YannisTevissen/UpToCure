# Adrenoleukodystrophy

## Overview

Adrenoleukodystrophy, usually X-linked adrenoleukodystrophy, is a rare genetic disease caused by loss-of-function variants in the ABCD1 gene, which impairs peroxisomal handling of very-long-chain fatty acids and is associated with adrenal insufficiency, progressive spinal cord disease called adrenomyeloneuropathy, and inflammatory brain demyelination called cerebral adrenoleukodystrophy [NEJM review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11431594/). It primarily affects males with an ABCD1 variant, but many female carriers develop adult spinal cord symptoms, and the same family mutation can lead to very different disease courses [NEJM review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11431594/). Newborn screening can detect affected infants before symptoms by measuring C26:0-lysophosphatidylcholine and confirming ABCD1 variants, but screen-positive children still need long-term endocrine and brain-MRI surveillance because symptoms may begin years later [newborn screening review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10302956/).

The most dangerous childhood form is early active cerebral adrenoleukodystrophy, which can cause loss of communication, vision, walking, feeding, continence, and voluntary movement; untreated cerebral disease is rapidly progressive and often fatal within years [FDA Skysona approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapy-treat-children-rare-disease). Current standard care is not one single cure: adrenal insufficiency is treated with hormone replacement when present, boys and men undergo scheduled MRI monitoring, early cerebral disease may be treated with allogeneic hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation or autologous gene-modified stem-cell therapy, and there is still no approved disease-modifying treatment for the adult spinal cord form, adrenomyeloneuropathy [consensus guidelines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35248114/) [ADVANCE trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39817991/).

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Since 2020, adrenoleukodystrophy research has been active but uneven: the field has made the most curative progress for early active cerebral disease through hematopoietic stem-cell approaches, while adult myelopathy and adrenal disease remain without restorative therapies [gene therapy review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9626408/). Dominant questions are how to identify cerebral disease early enough, how to deliver ABCD1 safely to the right long-lived cells, how to avoid transplant and vector-related toxicities, and whether brain-penetrant small molecules can slow disease before irreversible neurologic injury [ABCD1 targeting review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10136118/). The community is closest to a functional cure for early cerebral disease, meaning durable arrest of neurologic decline, but not yet a whole-disease cure that prevents adrenal failure, spinal cord degeneration, and all late complications [FDA Skysona label](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/skysona).

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

Gene-modified autologous hematopoietic stem-cell therapy has been the main breakthrough of the recent period. Elivaldogene autotemcel, also called eli-cel or Skysona, uses a lentiviral vector to add functional ABCD1 copies to a patient’s own CD34-positive blood stem cells, which are returned after myeloablative conditioning so their descendants can repopulate blood and brain immune-cell lineages [NEJM eli-cel study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36018074/). In the pivotal NEJM report, 29 of 32 treated boys were alive without major functional disabilities at 24 months, overall survival was 94%, and no graft failure or graft rejection was reported, although one acute myeloid leukemia case was considered likely related to vector insertion [NEJM eli-cel study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36018074/).

The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved Skysona in 2022 for boys 4–17 years old with early, active cerebral adrenoleukodystrophy, making it the first FDA-approved therapy shown to slow neurologic dysfunction in this disease [FDA Skysona approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapy-treat-children-rare-disease). The approval is narrow because Skysona is indicated for asymptomatic or mildly symptomatic boys with gadolinium-enhancing MRI lesions and Loes scores of 0.5–9, and the label states that it does not treat adrenal insufficiency [FDA Skysona label](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/skysona). Europe granted Skysona marketing authorization in 2021 but the authorization was withdrawn at the company’s request later that year, highlighting that regulatory success does not automatically solve manufacturing, access, or commercial sustainability [EMA Skysona page](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/skysona).

Safety has become the central limitation of lentiviral stem-cell gene therapy. A 2024 phase 3 ALD-104 publication reported that 29 of 32 evaluable eli-cel-treated boys survived without major functional disability at 24 months compared with 6 of 16 natural-history controls, but six treated patients developed hematologic malignancies, including myelodysplastic syndrome and acute myeloid leukemia, among those receiving the earlier Lenti-D vector product [ALD-104 results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38907607/). A 2024 mechanistic analysis found myelodysplastic syndrome in 7 of 67 eli-cel-treated patients and linked cases to vector insertional mutagenesis involving MECOM and PRDM16 activation, which has redirected the field toward safer vector design, monitoring for clonal expansion, and careful patient selection [insertional mutagenesis study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38598467/). The current U.S. prescribing information carries a boxed warning for hematologic malignancy and requires lifelong monitoring [DailyMed Skysona label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=fbf6b25a-9286-4a55-9b64-c2fcd1e65b59).

Allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation remains a proven disease-arresting strategy for early cerebral adrenoleukodystrophy when performed before advanced neurologic deficits. A large transplant outcomes study found that 5-year survival and major-functional-disability-free survival were higher when Loes MRI scores were below 10 and when patients had no neurologic deficit at transplant, confirming that timing is crucial [HCT outcomes study](https://www.astctjournal.org/article/S2666-6367(21)01288-3/fulltext). Compared with autologous gene therapy, allogeneic transplantation avoids lentiviral insertion risk but can involve donor availability, graft-versus-host disease, graft failure, and transplant-related morbidity [NEJM eli-cel study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36018074/).

Small-molecule strategies aim to modify disease biology rather than replace ABCD1 directly. Leriglitazone is an oral brain-penetrant PPAR-gamma agonist designed to influence mitochondrial function, oxidative stress, neuroinflammation, and ABCD2, a compensatory peroxisomal transporter [leriglitazone mechanism study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35500788/). In the phase 2/3 ADVANCE trial in adult men with adrenomyeloneuropathy, leriglitazone did not meet its primary 6-minute-walk endpoint but showed signals on Expanded Disability Status Scale progression and possible reduction in brain-lesion development, with weight gain and edema more common than placebo [ADVANCE trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39817991/). NEXUS is testing whether leriglitazone can arrest early cerebral disease in boys, with the primary endpoint defined as survival without major functional disabilities, arrest of gadolinium enhancement, and Neurologic Function Score of 1 or less at 96 weeks [NEXUS design paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39222769/).

Preclinical replacement and repair approaches are broadening beyond ex vivo stem cells. Intravenous AAV9-ABCD1 in Abcd1-deficient mice restored spinal cord ABCD1 expression, corrected very-long-chain fatty acid levels, and corrected patient induced-pluripotent-stem-cell-derived brain macrophages in culture, but did not improve myelopathy-like behavior in mice, showing that biochemical correction may not be enough to reverse established spinal cord disease [AAV9-ABCD1 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39633195/). Sobetirome prodrugs are another experimental approach; in mouse studies, CNS-targeted prodrugs increased brain and spinal cord sobetirome exposure and reduced brain very-long-chain fatty acids, but these remain preclinical for adrenoleukodystrophy [sobetirome prodrug study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10585740/).

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

The key recent eli-cel trials are ALD-102 and ALD-104. ALD-102 was a bluebird bio phase 2/3 study of autologous hematopoietic stem cells transduced ex vivo with the Lenti-D vector, enrolled 36 participants, and used 24-month survival without major functional disabilities as the primary endpoint [ALD-102 registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01896102). ALD-104 is a bluebird bio phase 3 study of eli-cel manufactured with the BB305 lentiviral vector, enrolled 35 participants, and had a 24-month major-functional-disability-free survival endpoint [ALD-104 registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03852498). Reported ALD-104 results support neurologic stabilization in most boys with early active cerebral disease, but malignancy findings mean that benefit-risk decisions remain individualized [ALD-104 results](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38907607/).

For small molecules, ADVANCE was a Minoryx-sponsored randomized placebo-controlled phase 2/3 trial in 116 adult men with adrenomyeloneuropathy, with 96-week motor and walking endpoints [ADVANCE registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03231878). NEXUS is a Minoryx-sponsored open-label phase 2/3 pediatric cerebral adrenoleukodystrophy study that began in 2020, enrolls boys with gadolinium-enhancing lesions and Loes scores 0.5–12, and is designed to evaluate disease arrest at week 96 [NEXUS registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04528706) [NEXUS design paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39222769/). These trials are important because they test whether an oral drug can help patients who are not ideal transplant candidates or can intervene at an earlier inflammatory stage [leriglitazone review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10805838/).

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Researchers are using newborn screening, serial MRI, neurologic function scoring, Loes lesion scoring, adrenal testing, plasma lipid biomarkers, vector-integration analysis, and long-term malignancy surveillance to connect early diagnosis with timely intervention and safety follow-up [newborn screening review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10302956/) [DailyMed Skysona label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=fbf6b25a-9286-4a55-9b64-c2fcd1e65b59). Clinical trials frequently use survival without major functional disability because the most meaningful goal for cerebral disease is preserving communication, vision, mobility, feeding, continence, and voluntary movement [FDA Skysona approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapy-treat-children-rare-disease).

Preclinical work uses Abcd1-deficient mice, patient fibroblasts, induced-pluripotent-stem-cell-derived brain macrophages, AAV vectors, lentiviral hematopoietic stem-cell platforms, and CNS-penetrant small molecules to test whether biochemical correction reaches the relevant brain, spinal cord, and immune cells [AAV9-ABCD1 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39633195/) [leriglitazone mechanism study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35500788/). A major methodological lesson from recent studies is that lowering very-long-chain fatty acids or restoring ABCD1 expression is necessary but may not be sufficient, especially for established spinal cord disease where axonal damage may be difficult to reverse [AAV9-ABCD1 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39633195/).

## Leading Institutions and Funding

The translational ecosystem includes academic leukodystrophy centers, transplant programs, biotechnology companies, patient foundations, and government funders. bluebird bio sponsored the ALD-102 and ALD-104 eli-cel trials and manufactures Skysona [ALD-102 registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01896102) [FDA Skysona label](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/skysona). Minoryx Therapeutics sponsored the ADVANCE and NEXUS leriglitazone trials [ADVANCE registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03231878) [NEXUS registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04528706). The NIH/NINDS funded a Massachusetts General Hospital project titled “Gene Therapy to Increase Adrenoleukodystrophy Protein in Bone Marrow Stem Cells for Adrenomyeloneuropathy” from 2021 to 2024 with $752,721 in listed funding [NIH grant record](https://grantome.com/grant/NIH/R61NS124610).

Patient-centered organizations are also shaping endpoints and trial readiness. ALD Connect awarded a 2024 research grant of $60,000 to Kennedy Krieger neurologist Gerald Raymond to validate urinary and patient-centered symptom measures for adrenomyeloneuropathy, reflecting the field’s need for sensitive tools in slow adult spinal cord disease [ALD Connect grant](https://aldconnect.org/news/ald-connect-awards-2024-research-grant-to-dr-gerald-raymond-for-amn-study/). Expert guideline efforts involving international ALD clinicians have also standardized MRI surveillance, transplant timing, and endocrine management recommendations, which is essential for comparing outcomes across centers [consensus guidelines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35248114/).

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The strongest recent achievement is that early cerebral adrenoleukodystrophy can now sometimes be halted before severe disability through transplantation or gene-modified autologous stem cells [HCT outcomes study](https://www.astctjournal.org/article/S2666-6367(21)01288-3/fulltext) [NEJM eli-cel study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36018074/). The biggest limitation is that these interventions require early detection, specialized centers, myeloablative conditioning, and careful risk selection, and they do not correct adrenal insufficiency or clearly prevent adult myelopathy [FDA Skysona label](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/skysona) [consensus guidelines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35248114/).

The safety challenge is substantial because lentiviral insertional mutagenesis has caused hematologic malignancies after eli-cel, requiring lifelong blood monitoring and driving the need for safer vectors or non-integrating approaches [insertional mutagenesis study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38598467/) [DailyMed Skysona label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=fbf6b25a-9286-4a55-9b64-c2fcd1e65b59). For adrenomyeloneuropathy, the challenge is different: progression is slow, endpoints are difficult, and the first large leriglitazone trial missed its primary walking endpoint despite supportive secondary signals [ADVANCE trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39817991/). Access is another unresolved issue because newborn screening coverage, MRI follow-up capacity, transplant expertise, and gene-therapy availability vary by region [newborn screening review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10302956/).

## Outlook and Future Directions

A true universal cure for adrenoleukodystrophy is not imminent, but a functional cure for carefully selected early cerebral disease is already partly achievable and should improve as newborn screening, MRI surveillance, safer vector engineering, and earlier treatment pathways mature [gene therapy review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9626408/) [newborn screening review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10302956/). Key milestones to watch are longer ALD-104 safety follow-up, whether BB305-manufactured Skysona shows lower genotoxicity than earlier Lenti-D products, NEXUS results for leriglitazone in pediatric cerebral disease, better endpoints for adult adrenomyeloneuropathy, and preclinical progress in AAV, CNS-targeted small molecules, and eventually more precise ABCD1 correction strategies [ALD-104 registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03852498) [NEXUS registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04528706) [AAV9-ABCD1 study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39633195/) [sobetirome prodrug study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10585740/).
