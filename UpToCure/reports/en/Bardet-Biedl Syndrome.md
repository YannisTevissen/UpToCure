# Bardet-Biedl Syndrome

## Overview

Bardet-Biedl syndrome (BBS) is a rare inherited disorder in which faulty genes disrupt the function of primary cilia—tiny structures that help cells sense and respond to signals. It can affect many organs, most commonly causing progressive retinal degeneration and vision loss, early-onset obesity, extra fingers or toes, learning or developmental differences, genital or urinary-tract differences, and kidney disease. Symptoms and severity vary substantially between individuals, including within families. [GeneReviews overview](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1363/) [European BBS consensus](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-024-01634-7)

There is currently no cure for BBS or for its retinal degeneration. Standard care is lifelong, multidisciplinary management: genetic diagnosis and counseling; regular eye, kidney, metabolic, endocrine, and developmental monitoring; low-vision rehabilitation; and treatment of complications. For BBS-associated obesity, setmelanotide is approved in the United States for eligible adults and children aged 2 years and older, but it treats appetite and weight rather than correcting the underlying genetic disease or restoring lost vision. [European BBS consensus](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-024-01634-7) [FDA setmelanotide designation and approvals](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=700719)

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Research since 2020 has become more translational, with the strongest curative-oriented work focused on replacing defective BBS genes in the retina before photoreceptors—the light-sensing cells—are lost. The field is active but still small: investigators are testing gene-replacement approaches in animal models, preparing or beginning first-in-human BBS1 retinal gene therapy, and using patient-derived stem-cell models to identify drug targets that might slow retinal damage. A whole-body cure remains distant because BBS involves more than 26 genes and affects the eye, brain, appetite pathways, kidneys, and other organs; however, preserving vision through early, gene-specific treatment is now a realistic near-term objective. [European BBS consensus](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-024-01634-7) [AXIS clinical trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07269665)

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

The leading disease-modifying strategy is retinal gene augmentation: delivering a functional copy of a BBS gene to retinal cells, generally using an adeno-associated virus (AAV), a modified non-disease-causing virus used as a delivery vehicle. In a 2022 BBS10 mouse study, subretinal AAV gene therapy preserved important rod and cone retinal function and delayed retinal degeneration for much of the animals’ lifespan. This was a major proof of concept because BBS10 is among the most common BBS genes and because the treatment addressed the disease mechanism rather than only a symptom. [BBS10 mouse gene therapy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36700052/)

A parallel BBS1 program, AXV-101, is designed to deliver a codon-optimized functional human *BBS1* gene using AAV9. Preclinical work reported by its developers found that subretinal treatment preserved retinal structure and rod and cone responses in a BBS1-M390R mouse model; these results remain preclinical and include company-associated conference data rather than peer-reviewed human evidence. [AXV-101 preclinical poster](https://axoviatherapeutics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ASCGT2025_BBS1_poster_922.pdf) [AXIS clinical trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07269665)

Small-molecule neuroprotection is another emerging, but earlier-stage, approach. A 2025 study used retinal organoids—three-dimensional retina-like tissues grown from patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs)—from people with *BBS10* variants. The researchers found abnormal activation of the DNA-damage response and showed that inhibiting the checkpoint kinase 2 (CHK2) pathway improved retinal organization, cone-cell survival, and outer-segment maturation in the laboratory model. This does not repair the mutated gene and has not yet been tested in people with BBS, but it may point toward a treatment that delays photoreceptor loss or complements gene therapy. [CHK2 inhibition in BBS retinal organoids](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11937680/)

Setmelanotide represents an important clinical advance for one component of BBS, rather than a cure. It activates the melanocortin-4 receptor appetite pathway, which is impaired downstream of ciliary dysfunction in many people with BBS. In the pivotal phase 3 study, 32.3% of participants aged 12 years or older with BBS achieved at least 10% weight loss after 52 weeks; common adverse effects included skin darkening and injection-site redness. [Phase 3 setmelanotide trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36356613/) [FDA setmelanotide designation and approvals](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=700719)

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

The most direct BBS curative trial effort is the AXIS study of AXV-101 for *BBS1*-related retinal degeneration. The study is an early phase 1, first-in-human, dose-escalation trial sponsored by Axovia Therapeutics and is designed primarily to assess safety, tolerability, and appropriate dosing after subretinal administration. As of the ClinicalTrials.gov update posted March 31, 2026, the study was listed as not yet recruiting and had no results posted; therefore, there is not yet human evidence that the treatment preserves or restores vision. [AXIS clinical trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07269665)

Setmelanotide’s completed phase 3 trial remains the most mature BBS interventional evidence. It established clinically meaningful weight loss for some participants and supported regulatory approval, but it does not replace a BBS gene, reverse established retinal degeneration, or address all BBS manifestations. [Phase 3 setmelanotide trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36356613/) [FDA setmelanotide designation and approvals](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=700719)

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Researchers combine genotype-specific models with detailed measures of retinal health. In BBS gene-therapy experiments, investigators use genetically engineered mice, subretinal AAV delivery, optical coherence tomography (OCT) to measure retinal-layer thickness, and electroretinography (ERG) to measure electrical responses of rods and cones to light. These tests can show whether a therapy protects retinal cells structurally and functionally before proceeding to human studies. [BBS10 mouse gene therapy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36700052/) [AXV-101 preclinical poster](https://axoviatherapeutics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ASCGT2025_BBS1_poster_922.pdf)

Human iPSC models are increasingly important because they allow researchers to create patient-specific hypothalamic neurons and retinal organoids in the laboratory. BBS iPSC-derived neurons have helped connect BBS protein dysfunction to altered intracellular signaling relevant to appetite regulation, while BBS retinal organoids enable testing of cell-death pathways and candidate protective drugs in human genetic backgrounds. [BBS iPSC-derived neurons](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33630762/) [CHK2 inhibition in BBS retinal organoids](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11937680/)

## Leading Institutions and Funding

The University of Iowa, Massachusetts Eye and Ear/Harvard Medical School, and collaborating investigators produced the BBS10 mouse gene-therapy proof of concept. In the United Kingdom, Axovia Therapeutics, Brunel University London, University College London, and King’s College London are associated with the BBS1 AXV-101 program and its planned first-in-human study. [BBS10 mouse gene therapy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36700052/) [AXV-101 preclinical poster](https://axoviatherapeutics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ASCGT2025_BBS1_poster_922.pdf) [AXIS clinical trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07269665)

Patient-led infrastructure is unusually important in this very rare condition. The Bardet-Biedl Syndrome Foundation reports providing about $750,000 since 2013 to support the Clinical Registry Investigating Bardet-Biedl Syndrome (CRIBBS), which has enrolled more than 1,000 people internationally and supplies the natural-history and genotype information needed for trial design and recruitment. European Reference Networks have also coordinated multidisciplinary clinical recommendations intended to improve diagnosis, surveillance, and readiness for future innovative trials. [BBS Foundation clinical practice and CRIBBS](https://www.bardetbiedl.org/clinical-practice) [European BBS consensus](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-024-01634-7)

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The central strength of current research is that the retina is accessible for local treatment and can be monitored repeatedly with OCT, ERG, and functional vision tests. Animal results in BBS10 and BBS1 models support the idea that enough functional protein can be supplied to slow retinal degeneration, especially when treatment begins before extensive photoreceptor death. Human retinal-organoid work also offers a practical way to identify protective drugs in relevant patient cells. [BBS10 mouse gene therapy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36700052/) [AXV-101 preclinical poster](https://axoviatherapeutics.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/ASCGT2025_BBS1_poster_922.pdf) [CHK2 inhibition in BBS retinal organoids](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11937680/)

The largest limitations are genetic diversity, timing, and the multi-organ nature of BBS. A gene therapy for *BBS1* will not directly treat people whose disease is caused by *BBS10*, *BBS7*, or another BBS gene, and retinal treatment cannot by itself correct kidney disease, developmental differences, or obesity. AAV therapies may also face dose, immune-response, durability, surgical, and access challenges. Most importantly, existing retinal gene-therapy evidence in BBS is preclinical or in a trial without posted human outcomes, while international guidance still states that no specific therapy for BBS retinal degeneration is available. [European BBS consensus](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-024-01634-7) [AXIS clinical trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07269665)

## Outlook and Future Directions

BBS is not close to a whole-body cure as of August 8, 2026, but it has moved beyond purely symptomatic research: the key milestone to watch is the safety and early retinal-function data from the AXIS *BBS1* gene-therapy study. Positive human evidence of durable photoreceptor preservation would validate gene-specific treatment for BBS retinal degeneration and could accelerate analogous programs for *BBS10* and other genes. Equally important will be earlier genetic diagnosis, registry-based natural-history data, scalable retinal delivery, and treatments that can safely address non-eye manifestations; until then, setmelanotide and multidisciplinary care remain essential but non-curative components of treatment.
