# Acromegaly

## Overview

Acromegaly is a rare hormonal disorder in which prolonged excess growth hormone (GH) causes the liver and other tissues to produce too much insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1). In adults, this can enlarge the hands, feet, facial bones, and internal organs and can contribute to diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, sleep apnea, arthritis, headaches, and vision problems. More than 90% of cases are caused by a usually benign GH-secreting pituitary tumor, also called a somatotroph adenoma or pituitary neuroendocrine tumor. [NIDDK overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/acromegaly)

When effective treatment normalizes hormone excess, symptoms and health risks can improve substantially and life expectancy may return toward normal. The current standard of care is surgical removal of the pituitary tumor through the nose (transsphenoidal surgery) when feasible, followed when necessary by medicines that suppress GH release or block GH action, and sometimes focused radiation therapy. Modern consensus guidance uses the term “remission,” rather than an absolute cure, because biochemical tests cannot prove that all disease has been permanently eliminated. [Acromegaly remission consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10837217/) [NIDDK overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/acromegaly)

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Research since 2020 has been active but has primarily focused on improving durable hormone control, reducing treatment burden, refining surgical remission assessment, and matching therapy to tumor biology rather than developing a one-time molecular cure. The nearest existing curative approach remains complete removal of the GH-secreting tumor; the most important recent therapeutic advances are oral or self-administered medicines that improve control for people whose surgery is incomplete, unsafe, or unsuccessful. [Acromegaly remission consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10837217/) [FDA approval of paltusotine](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-new-treatment-acromegaly-rare-endocrine-disorder)

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

**More precise pursuit of surgical remission.** Surgery remains the only routinely available intervention that can remove the source of excess GH in a single procedure. Recent consensus work has strengthened how remission is assessed: IGF-1 is generally checked about 12 weeks after surgery, with GH testing, imaging, symptoms, and long-term follow-up used to identify persistent or recurrent disease. This matters for cure-oriented research because apparent early biochemical control can differ from durable remission, particularly for large or invasive tumors. [Acromegaly remission consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10837217/)

**Oral somatostatin-receptor targeting.** The major recent drug advance is paltusotine, an oral, selective somatostatin receptor type 2 agonist that suppresses GH secretion from many somatotroph tumors. The U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved paltusotine, marketed as Palsonify, on September 25, 2025 for adults with inadequate response to surgery or for whom surgery is not an option. This is not a cure: it is ongoing treatment designed to keep IGF-1 controlled. However, its daily oral delivery can replace or avoid some long-acting injections and may improve access to sustained biochemical control. [FDA approval of paltusotine](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-new-treatment-acromegaly-rare-endocrine-disorder) [FDA prescribing information](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/219070s000lbl.pdf)

**Long-acting self-administered depot treatment.** CAM2029 is a monthly subcutaneous octreotide depot designed for self-administration and room-temperature storage. In the phase 3 ACROINNOVA 1 trial, it maintained biochemical control more effectively than placebo among people previously controlled on injected standard therapies, while improving treatment satisfaction and quality-of-life measures. Like paltusotine, this is a treatment-delivery innovation rather than tumor eradication, but it may make long-term control more practical for people who are not surgically cured. [ACROINNOVA 1 phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39378125/)

**Precision medicine and tumor-targeted discovery.** Researchers are increasingly examining why somatotroph tumors behave differently and why some respond poorly to standard somatostatin-receptor drugs. RNA sequencing has identified molecularly distinct groups of tumors causing acromegaly, while genomic work indicates that broad chromosome-level copy-number changes and altered cyclic AMP and DNA-repair pathways may be more important than a single recurrent mutation in many tumors. These findings provide possible future treatment targets and biomarkers, but they have not yet produced a validated gene therapy, gene-editing treatment, RNA therapeutic, or cell therapy that cures acromegaly in people. [Transcriptomic classification of acromegaly tumors](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36497102/) [DNA damage and GH hypersecretion study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32673291/)

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

Crinetics Pharmaceuticals’ phase 3 PATHFNDR-1 trial tested oral paltusotine in 58 adults whose disease was already controlled with injected somatostatin-receptor ligands. After switching from injections, 25 of 30 participants receiving paltusotine (83.3%) maintained normal IGF-1 at 36 weeks, compared with 1 of 28 participants receiving placebo (3.6%). [PATHFNDR-1 phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38828555/) The related phase 3 PATHFNDR-2 trial enrolled 111 adults with uncontrolled acromegaly who were not receiving active medical treatment at randomization; at 24 weeks, normal IGF-1 was achieved by 30 of 54 participants receiving paltusotine (55.6%), versus 3 of 57 receiving placebo (5.3%). [PATHFNDR-2 phase 3 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13016795/)

Camurus-associated investigators tested CAM2029 in the phase 3 ACROINNOVA 1 study, which randomized 72 previously controlled participants to monthly CAM2029 or placebo after withdrawal of prior standard injectable therapy. At weeks 22–24, 72.2% of the CAM2029 group met the primary IGF-1 control endpoint, compared with 37.5% of the placebo group. [ACROINNOVA 1 phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39378125/) These trials demonstrate meaningful control of hormone excess, but neither was designed to show permanent tumor elimination after treatment stops. [FDA approval of paltusotine](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-new-treatment-acromegaly-rare-endocrine-disorder)

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Clinical studies use age-adjusted IGF-1 as the principal biomarker of disease activity, supported by GH measurements, symptom diaries, quality-of-life tools, pituitary magnetic resonance imaging, and monitoring for treatment-related adverse effects. The 2024 international consensus emphasizes interpreting hormone results alongside symptoms, tumor imaging, complications, and repeat testing when results are uncertain. [Acromegaly remission consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10837217/)

At the laboratory level, researchers combine resected human tumor tissue, RNA sequencing, quantitative gene-expression testing, genomic copy-number analysis, and primary pituitary-cell and mouse-pituitary models. These platforms are being used to identify tumor subtypes, predict drug responsiveness, and test whether pathways involved in cyclic AMP signaling, DNA damage, and hormone secretion can be therapeutically exploited. [Transcriptomic classification of acromegaly tumors](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36497102/) [DNA damage and GH hypersecretion study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32673291/)

## Leading Institutions and Funding

The field is driven by international pituitary centers and multicenter trial networks, including Massachusetts General Hospital, Stanford University, Columbia University, the University of Michigan, IRCCS Ospedale Policlinico San Martino in Genoa, and participating centers across Europe, the Americas, and Asia. These networks have been essential because acromegaly is rare and adequately powered trials require recruitment across many countries. [PATHFNDR-2 phase 3 trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13016795/) [ACROINNOVA 1 phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39378125/)

Commercial development has been particularly important for recent delivery innovations, with Crinetics Pharmaceuticals advancing paltusotine and Camurus participating in development of CAM2029. In the United States, the National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases supports endocrine and human-subjects research through investigator-initiated grants and multicenter clinical-research mechanisms, although publicly available program descriptions do not identify a single acromegaly-specific funding total. ## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The strongest feature of current progress is that patients now have more practical ways to achieve and maintain normal IGF-1, including a newly approved oral drug and potentially more convenient depot formulations. Better biochemical definitions of remission and multidisciplinary pituitary care also reduce the risk that persistent disease is missed after surgery. [FDA approval of paltusotine](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/news-events-human-drugs/fda-approves-new-treatment-acromegaly-rare-endocrine-disorder) [Acromegaly remission consensus](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10837217/)

The core limitation is that most new treatments suppress hormone production rather than eliminate the tumor, so they usually require continued use and monitoring. Not every patient reaches normal IGF-1 on paltusotine, and somatostatin-pathway treatment can cause gastrointestinal effects, gallstones, blood-glucose changes, slow heart rate, thyroid abnormalities, and nutrient-malabsorption concerns. Invasive tumors near critical structures may remain difficult to remove completely, while radiation may take years to work and can damage normal pituitary function. [FDA prescribing information](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/219070s000lbl.pdf) [NIDDK overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/acromegaly)

## Outlook and Future Directions

As of August 8, 2026, acromegaly is closer to more convenient lifelong control than to a broadly applicable molecular cure. The most meaningful milestones to watch are durable post-surgical remission in invasive tumors, validated molecular markers that predict which tumor will respond to which treatment, and preclinical evidence that a tumor-specific pathway can safely eliminate GH-secreting cells without damaging the normal pituitary. Until then, earlier diagnosis, referral to experienced multidisciplinary pituitary centers, complete safe tumor resection when possible, and personalized long-term hormone control remain the most realistic route to remission and restored health.
