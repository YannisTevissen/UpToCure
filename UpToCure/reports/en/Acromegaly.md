# Acromegaly

## Overview

Acromegaly is a rare endocrine disorder in which excess growth hormone, usually from a benign pituitary adenoma, raises insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1) and gradually causes enlarged hands, feet, facial bones, soft tissues, and organs, along with complications such as joint disease, sleep apnea, diabetes, hypertension, and cardiovascular risk ([NIDDK acromegaly overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/acromegaly)). It usually appears in adults and is often diagnosed late because physical changes develop slowly over years ([NIDDK acromegaly overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/acromegaly)).

The current curative-intent treatment is expert transsphenoidal surgery to remove the growth-hormone–secreting pituitary tumor when that can be done safely, while medicines and radiotherapy are used when surgery is incomplete, unsafe, or followed by persistent biochemical disease ([Pituitary Society management update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00554-0)). Standard drug treatment suppresses growth-hormone secretion with somatostatin receptor ligands, lowers hormone output with selected dopamine agonist use, or blocks growth-hormone action with pegvisomant; these approaches can normalize IGF-1 and symptoms but usually control rather than eliminate the adenoma ([Pituitary Society management update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00554-0)).

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

From 2020 through 2026, acromegaly research has been active but has mainly pursued better disease control rather than a one-time biological cure, with major work on oral somatostatin-pathway drugs, improved long-acting depots, biochemical remission criteria, and patient-centered outcomes such as symptoms, convenience, and treatment satisfaction ([MPOWERED oral octreotide trial](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(21)00296-5/fulltext), [PATHFNDR-1 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04837040), [CAM2029 ACROINNOVA trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04076462), [Therapeutic outcomes consensus update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-025-01148-2)). The community is close to more convenient chronic therapy for many patients, but not close to a broadly applicable non-surgical cure that selectively removes or permanently silences pituitary adenomas ([Pituitary Society management update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00554-0), [Therapeutic outcomes consensus update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-025-01148-2)).

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

The most important recent breakthrough has been oral maintenance therapy for patients already responsive to injectable somatostatin receptor ligands. Oral octreotide capsules were approved in 2020 for long-term maintenance treatment in adults with acromegaly who responded to and tolerated octreotide or lanreotide, turning a traditionally injectable pathway into a twice-daily oral option for selected patients ([FDA Mycapssa label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/208232s000lbl.pdf)). The phase 3 MPOWERED trial compared oral octreotide capsules with injectable somatostatin receptor ligands in patients who had already shown response to both approaches, and it focused on maintenance of biochemical response and symptom control rather than tumor eradication ([MPOWERED oral octreotide trial](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(21)00296-5/fulltext)).

A second major advance is paltusotine, a once-daily oral, non-peptide somatostatin receptor 2 agonist designed to mimic the key receptor effect of injectable first-generation somatostatin analogs without depot injections. In 2025, paltusotine was reported as FDA-approved for adults with acromegaly for whom surgery was not an option or had not been curative, making it the first once-daily oral treatment in this class ([Paltusotine FDA approval commentary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12768094/)). The phase 3 PATHFNDR-1 trial studied adults controlled on injectable somatostatin receptor ligands before switching to paltusotine or placebo, while PATHFNDR-2 studied adults with acromegaly who were not biochemically controlled and therefore addressed a more difficult clinical population ([PATHFNDR-1 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04837040), [PATHFNDR-2 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05192382)).

A third strategy is improved long-acting delivery of known peptide drugs. CAM2029 is an investigational subcutaneous octreotide depot being tested as an alternative to older long-acting somatostatin injections, with phase 3 ACROINNOVA trials designed to evaluate biochemical control, symptoms, safety, and longer-term treatment experience in adults with acromegaly ([CAM2029 ACROINNOVA trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04076462), [CAM2029 extension trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05050942)). This strategy is not a cure, but it could reduce injection burden and improve adherence for patients who need years of medical therapy after surgery or instead of surgery ([CAM2029 ACROINNOVA trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04076462)).

Surgery remains the only widely used curative-intent therapy because it directly removes the growth-hormone–secreting tumor, and recent medical advances are best understood as complements for patients with persistent, recurrent, invasive, or unresectable disease ([Pituitary Society management update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00554-0)). Modern consensus work has also emphasized standardized outcomes, including IGF-1 normalization, growth-hormone targets, tumor control, comorbidity improvement, and quality of life, so that new therapies can be compared more consistently across trials and clinics ([Therapeutic outcomes consensus update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-025-01148-2)).

No gene therapy, gene-editing therapy, RNA therapeutic, or cell therapy is established as a curative treatment for acromegaly in routine care as of May 2026. The main reason is that most acromegaly is caused by a localized pituitary adenoma rather than a single body-wide missing gene or cell type, so today’s curative logic remains tumor removal or durable tumor control rather than molecular replacement ([NIDDK acromegaly overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/acromegaly), [Pituitary Society management update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00554-0)).

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

The key oral-octreotide program includes OPTIMAL and MPOWERED. OPTIMAL was a phase 3 trial of oral octreotide capsules in adults with acromegaly previously controlled on injectable somatostatin receptor ligands, and MPOWERED was a phase 3 randomized comparison of oral octreotide capsules with continued injectable somatostatin receptor ligands in patients who had demonstrated biochemical response ([OPTIMAL oral octreotide trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03252353), [MPOWERED oral octreotide trial](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(21)00296-5/fulltext)). These studies supported a maintenance-treatment role for oral octreotide capsules, not a claim that the drug cures pituitary adenomas ([FDA Mycapssa label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/208232s000lbl.pdf)).

Paltusotine’s PATHFNDR program is among the most important recent experimental programs because it tests once-daily oral somatostatin receptor 2 activation in both previously controlled and uncontrolled acromegaly. PATHFNDR-1 enrolled patients controlled on injectable somatostatin receptor ligands and randomized them to paltusotine or placebo, while PATHFNDR-2 evaluated paltusotine in adults with active acromegaly who were not adequately controlled at baseline ([PATHFNDR-1 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04837040), [PATHFNDR-2 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05192382)).

CAM2029 is being tested in phase 3 trials as a new subcutaneous octreotide depot. ACROINNOVA evaluates efficacy and safety in adults with acromegaly, and an extension study follows longer-term treatment and safety after participation in earlier CAM2029 studies ([CAM2029 ACROINNOVA trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04076462), [CAM2029 extension trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05050942)).

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Researchers measure possible “cure” or control with biochemical markers, especially IGF-1, because IGF-1 reflects integrated growth-hormone exposure and is central to remission and treatment-response definitions ([Therapeutic outcomes consensus update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-025-01148-2)). Trials also follow growth-hormone levels, symptoms, adverse events, patient-reported treatment burden, rescue-medication rules, and pituitary MRI when tumor size or stability is clinically relevant ([MPOWERED oral octreotide trial](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(21)00296-5/fulltext), [PATHFNDR-1 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04837040), [CAM2029 ACROINNOVA trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04076462)).

The main recent trial designs are maintenance-switch studies, randomized withdrawal studies, placebo-controlled phase 3 trials, and long-term extension studies, because many patients enter trials after prior surgery or stable injectable therapy rather than at the first moment of diagnosis ([OPTIMAL oral octreotide trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03252353), [PATHFNDR-1 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04837040), [CAM2029 extension trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05050942)). Scientific work is also moving toward better response prediction, because somatostatin-pathway drugs work best when the tumor biology and receptor profile make hormone suppression achievable ([Pituitary Society management update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00554-0)).

## Leading Institutions and Funding

Specialized pituitary centers and endocrine-surgery programs remain central because surgery is the main curative-intent treatment and because long-term monitoring requires coordinated endocrinology, neurosurgery, radiology, and radiation-oncology expertise ([Pituitary Society management update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00554-0)). Major industry programs since 2020 include Chiasma/Amryt’s oral octreotide capsule program, Crinetics Pharmaceuticals’ PATHFNDR paltusotine program, and Camurus’s ACROINNOVA CAM2029 program, each of which has used phase 3 trials to support regulatory or late-stage development decisions ([FDA Mycapssa label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/208232s000lbl.pdf), [PATHFNDR-1 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04837040), [CAM2029 ACROINNOVA trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04076462)).

Public and nonprofit infrastructure is also important even when individual pivotal trials are company sponsored. The National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases provides patient-facing disease education, the FDA evaluates drug indications and labeling, ClinicalTrials.gov provides public trial registration, and international expert groups such as the Pituitary Society and acromegaly consensus panels define outcomes that guide trial design and clinical practice ([NIDDK acromegaly overview](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/endocrine-diseases/acromegaly), [FDA Mycapssa label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/208232s000lbl.pdf), [Therapeutic outcomes consensus update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-025-01148-2)).

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The field’s strengths are unusually clear biomarkers, a visible tumor target, established surgical expertise, and multiple validated hormone-pathway therapies, which together make it possible to test whether a new drug keeps IGF-1 controlled and symptoms stable in relatively focused trials ([Therapeutic outcomes consensus update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-025-01148-2)). Recent oral and depot approaches also address a real daily-life problem: many patients live for years with injections, breakthrough symptoms, monitoring visits, and uncertainty after incomplete tumor removal ([MPOWERED oral octreotide trial](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/landia/article/PIIS2213-8587(21)00296-5/fulltext), [CAM2029 ACROINNOVA trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04076462)).

The main limitation is that most recent advances improve chronic control rather than cure the disease. Oral octreotide, paltusotine, and CAM2029 can reduce treatment burden or improve biochemical control in selected patients, but they do not reliably erase the pituitary adenoma, and durable remission remains difficult for large or invasive tumors that cannot be fully removed safely ([FDA Mycapssa label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2020/208232s000lbl.pdf), [PATHFNDR-1 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04837040), [Pituitary Society management update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00554-0)).

## Outlook and Future Directions

A universal cure for acromegaly is unlikely in the near term, but meaningful progress is likely through earlier diagnosis, referral to high-volume pituitary surgery teams, better prediction of drug response, wider use of oral options for appropriate patients, and longer-term data on paltusotine and CAM2029 ([Pituitary Society management update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00554-0), [PATHFNDR-2 paltusotine trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05192382), [CAM2029 extension trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05050942)). The milestones to watch are sustained IGF-1 normalization without injections, proof that convenience improves adherence and outcomes, safer management of invasive tumors, and any future tumor-targeted strategy that can permanently stop growth-hormone excess without damaging normal pituitary function ([Therapeutic outcomes consensus update](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-025-01148-2)).
