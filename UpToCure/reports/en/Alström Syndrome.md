# Alström Syndrome

## Overview

Alström syndrome is an ultra-rare, inherited condition caused by harmful variants in both copies of the *ALMS1* gene. It affects many body systems: children commonly develop early vision loss from cone-rod retinal degeneration, and many later develop hearing loss, obesity, severe insulin resistance and type 2 diabetes, heart muscle disease, fatty liver disease, kidney dysfunction, and fibrosis (scarring) in several organs. Symptoms usually begin in infancy or childhood but vary widely between individuals; progression of heart, kidney, liver, and metabolic disease is the main determinant of long-term health. [Consensus management guidelines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32958032/) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32958032/))

There is no cure or approved treatment that restores normal *ALMS1* function. Current standard care is proactive, lifelong multidisciplinary management: surveillance and treatment for cardiomyopathy, diabetes and dyslipidaemia, kidney and liver disease, hearing and vision support, nutrition and weight management, and treatment of endocrine or respiratory complications. Earlier diagnosis and coordinated specialist care can slow complications and improve quality of life. [Consensus management guidelines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32958032/) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32958032/))

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Research since 2020 has been active but remains small because Alström syndrome affects roughly one person per million births and has a limited patient population for trials. The field is moving on two tracks: finding treatments that reduce dangerous metabolic and fibrotic complications now, and developing approaches that address the underlying *ALMS1* defect. The latter remains early-stage: no gene-replacement, gene-editing, RNA, or cell therapy has yet entered an Alström-specific clinical trial, and the large size of *ALMS1* is a major delivery challenge for conventional gene-therapy vectors. [Consensus management guidelines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32958032/) ([alstrom.org.uk](https://alstrom.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Clinical-Guidelines-2020.pdf?utm_source=openai))

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

The most directly disease-targeted advance is **translational readthrough therapy** for people with nonsense variants—DNA changes that create a premature “stop” signal and truncate the ALMS1 protein. In 2021, researchers treated patient-derived skin fibroblasts with ataluren (PTC124) and amlexanox. Both compounds restored detectable full-length ALMS1 protein in tested cells; the study also found improved localization of ciliary proteins and correction of a ciliary signaling defect in cells carrying a specific homozygous *ALMS1* nonsense variant. This is an important proof of principle, but it is not yet evidence of patient benefit, and it would apply only to the subset of patients whose variants are amenable to readthrough. [Readthrough therapy in patient fibroblasts](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34365092/) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34365092/?utm_source=openai))

A second emerging strategy is identifying pathways downstream of ALMS1 loss that could be drugged even when direct gene correction is not yet feasible. A 2025 CRISPR-Cas9 knockout and phosphoproteomics study found that ALMS1 deficiency disrupted transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) signaling, a pathway strongly associated with fibrosis, and identified CDC42 as a central candidate regulator in ALMS1-deficient cells. These findings do not establish a treatment, but they provide a rational route for testing anti-fibrotic or pathway-modulating drugs in Alström models. [ALMS1 phosphoproteomics and TGF-β signaling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589413/) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589413/?utm_source=openai))

Human “disease-in-a-dish” models are also refining therapeutic targets for cardiomyopathy. In 2024, investigators used induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes—heart cells made from engineered human stem cells lacking *ALMS1*—and reported altered calcium handling, higher metabolic demand, and increased cellular senescence. These abnormalities offer measurable laboratory outcomes for testing medicines aimed at protecting the heart before irreversible fibrosis or heart failure develops. [ALMS1-knockout cardiomyocyte model](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1096719224004591) ([sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1096719224004591?utm_source=openai))

Metabolic medicines are not cures, but they are increasingly important disease-modifying supportive therapies. In a 2024 real-world cohort of 30 adults with Alström syndrome, six months of the glucagon-like peptide-1 receptor agonists semaglutide or exenatide was associated with an average 5.4 kg weight reduction and a 1.1-percentage-point reduction in HbA1c, a measure of average blood glucose. [GLP-1 receptor agonists in Alström syndrome](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38151964/) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38151964/?utm_source=openai)) A 2025 report of two individuals treated with tirzepatide, which activates both GIP and GLP-1 receptors, described substantial weight loss, lower insulin requirements, improved hyperphagia, and reduced liver fat; however, this was an uncontrolled two-person case series and cannot establish efficacy or safety for the broader population. [Tirzepatide case series](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/110/12/3364/8122500) ([academic.oup.com](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/110/12/3364/8122500?utm_source=openai))

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

The principal recent interventional trial was the completed Phase 3 study of the melanocortin-4 receptor agonist setmelanotide, sponsored by Rhythm Pharmaceuticals: [NCT03746522](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03746522). It enrolled participants with Bardet-Biedl syndrome or Alström syndrome and obesity. Although the combined study met its main endpoint, all participants achieving at least 10% weight loss at one year had Bardet-Biedl syndrome; results in the Alström subgroup were not statistically significant. Skin hyperpigmentation was the most frequently reported adverse event. Thus, setmelanotide did not become an evidence-based disease-specific treatment for Alström syndrome. [Phase 3 setmelanotide trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9847480/) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9847480/?utm_source=openai))

The Strasbourg University Hospital observational study, [NCT02890550](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02890550), was designed to characterize genetic, sensory, metabolic, kidney, cardiac, lung, and reproductive manifestations and to collect biospecimens for research. The registry lists it as terminated, with no posted results. Its planned use of clinical data, blood, urine, skin, and adipose samples illustrates the field’s need for longitudinal natural-history data and human biospecimens to support future therapeutic trials. [Strasbourg natural-history study](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02890550) ([clinicaltrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02890550?utm_source=openai))

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Researchers combine patient-derived fibroblasts, CRISPR-engineered cell lines, induced pluripotent stem cell-derived heart cells, and mouse models. Patient fibroblasts are particularly useful for testing variant-specific approaches such as nonsense-codon readthrough, whereas gene-edited cells enable controlled comparison of ALMS1-deficient and normal cells. Proteomics and phosphoproteomics are being used to map disturbed signaling networks, including TGF-β-related pathways, while cardiomyocyte models provide functional measurements such as contraction, calcium cycling, energy use, and senescence. [Readthrough therapy in patient fibroblasts](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34365092/) [ALMS1 phosphoproteomics and TGF-β signaling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589413/) [ALMS1-knockout cardiomyocyte model](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1096719224004591) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34365092/?utm_source=openai))

Clinical research is increasingly using organ-specific biomarkers to detect progression before symptoms become severe. Examples include HbA1c and body weight for metabolic disease, magnetic resonance imaging for liver fat, and cardiac magnetic resonance measures of diffuse myocardial fibrosis. In adults with Alström syndrome, serial cardiac magnetic resonance imaging has shown silent progression of diffuse interstitial heart fibrosis, supporting its use as a biomarker for prevention-focused studies. [Cardiac MRI fibrosis study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32503575/) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32503575/?utm_source=openai))

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The field’s principal strengths are that Alström syndrome is caused by one known gene, many disease-causing variants are loss-of-function variants, and patient-cell and animal models can reproduce meaningful aspects of ciliary, metabolic, kidney, and heart disease. The readthrough experiments show that some mutant cells can regain full-length ALMS1 protein and ciliary function, while newer cell models reveal measurable pathways and biomarkers for testing candidate medicines. [Readthrough therapy in patient fibroblasts](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34365092/) [ALMS1 phosphoproteomics and TGF-β signaling](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12589413/) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34365092/?utm_source=openai))

The limitations are substantial. ALMS1 is a very large gene, making whole-gene delivery difficult; it is needed across many tissues; and vision, hearing, heart, kidney, liver, and metabolic complications may already be partly irreversible when treatment begins. Readthrough compounds are mutation-specific and must prove durable protein restoration and safety in relevant organs. Meanwhile, metabolic drugs can reduce weight, blood glucose, insulin needs, and liver fat, but they do not replace ALMS1 or reliably prevent sensory degeneration and fibrosis. Evidence for newer medicines such as tirzepatide remains limited to very small, uncontrolled studies. [Consensus management guidelines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32958032/) [Tirzepatide case series](https://academic.oup.com/jcem/article/110/12/3364/8122500) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32958032/))

## Outlook and Future Directions

As of August 8, 2026, a true cure for Alström syndrome is not close to clinical use, but the route toward one is becoming clearer. The most important milestones to watch are replication of nonsense-readthrough results in additional patient variants and disease-relevant tissues; demonstration that candidate anti-fibrotic or cilia-restoring treatments work in animal models; development of delivery systems capable of safely reaching multiple ALMS1-affected organs; and prospective trials that use sensitive heart, kidney, liver, metabolic, and sensory biomarkers. In the nearer term, better use of incretin-based medicines and rigorous studies of fibrosis-modifying therapies may reduce morbidity while root-cause treatments mature.
