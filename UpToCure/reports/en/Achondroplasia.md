# Achondroplasia

## Overview

Achondroplasia is the most common skeletal dysplasia, a genetic condition in which an activating variant in **FGFR3**—a gene that normally restrains bone growth—slows the formation of bone from cartilage, particularly in the limbs and spine. It causes disproportionate short stature and can also affect the skull base, spinal canal, ear function, breathing, leg alignment, and joints. Clinical impact varies substantially between people and across the lifespan. [International consensus statement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00595-x)

Prognosis is therefore shaped less by height alone than by timely recognition and treatment of complications. Current standard care is lifelong, multidisciplinary surveillance by clinicians experienced in skeletal dysplasias, including monitoring of growth and head size with achondroplasia-specific charts, neurologic and sleep-breathing assessment, hearing care, orthopedic care, and surgery when indicated for problems such as craniocervical compression or symptomatic spinal stenosis. [GeneReviews: Achondroplasia](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1152/?report=classic) In the United States, the disease-modifying medicines currently approved for children with open growth plates are vosoritide, a daily injection, and navepegritide, a weekly injection; neither corrects the causal DNA variant. [FDA approval of vosoritide](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-improve-growth-children-most-common-form-dwarfism) [FDA approval letter for navepegritide](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/219164Orig1s000ltr.pdf)

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Research since 2020 has been active and increasingly mechanism-driven: the principal questions are how early FGFR3 overactivity should be treated, whether growth and body proportions can be improved safely over many years, and whether medical treatment can reduce clinically meaningful complications as well as increase height. [International consensus statement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00595-x) The field has advanced from supportive care alone to multiple targeted medicines in or near clinical use, but it is **not close to a genetic cure**: no human trial has yet attempted to permanently correct the FGFR3 variant throughout a person’s developing skeleton. [Targeted gene correction in patient-derived iPSCs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8403427/)

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

**C-type natriuretic peptide (CNP) pathway medicines.** CNP is a natural signaling molecule that can counter excessive FGFR3 signaling in growth-plate cartilage. Vosoritide, a modified CNP analogue, was the first major clinical breakthrough: in a randomized phase 3 trial, children receiving daily injections had an adjusted annualized growth-velocity advantage of 1.57 cm per year over placebo. [Vosoritide phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32891212/) The FDA first approved vosoritide in November 2021 and expanded its pediatric indication in October 2023 to children with open growth plates regardless of age. [FDA approval of vosoritide](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-improve-growth-children-most-common-form-dwarfism) [FDA orphan-drug record](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=386012)

A longer-acting CNP prodrug, **navepegritide** (formerly TransCon CNP), was designed for once-weekly dosing. In its phase 2 randomized trial, the 100 μg/kg/week dose increased annualized growth velocity at 52 weeks versus placebo, while reducing the burden of injections relative to daily treatment. [ACcomplisH phase 2 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37823031/) The FDA granted accelerated approval for navepegritide, marketed as Yuviwel, on February 27, 2026 for children aged two years and older with open growth plates; the approved study showed a 1.5 cm/year treatment difference in annualized growth velocity after 52 weeks. [FDA approval letter for navepegritide](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/219164Orig1s000ltr.pdf) These medicines modify a downstream growth pathway while treatment continues; they do not remove or repair the FGFR3 variant.

**Direct FGFR inhibition.** Infigratinib is a once-daily oral small-molecule inhibitor of FGFR1–3, intended to suppress signaling closer to the root molecular defect. [Infigratinib phase 2 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555818/) In the highest-dose cohort of its phase 2 study, annualized height velocity increased by a mean 2.50 cm/year from baseline at 18 months, with improvement in height z score and in the upper-to-lower body-segment ratio; all participants had adverse events, but none discontinued treatment because of them. [Infigratinib phase 2 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555818/) A subsequently reported phase 3 placebo-controlled trial found a 1.74 cm/year between-group difference in change in annualized height velocity at week 52, but longer follow-up is needed to establish effects on adult height, complications, and long-term safety. [Infigratinib phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42370681/)

**Next-generation long-acting CNP.** BioMarin’s investigational **BMN 333** is a long-acting CNP programme intended to provide sustained exposure with less frequent administration. Its registration-enabling active-controlled phase 2/3 study, ASPEN, compares BMN 333 with vosoritide in treatment-naive children, using annualized growth velocity as the principal efficacy measure. [ASPEN trial registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07441876) This is an incremental delivery and exposure strategy rather than a curative genetic intervention. [BMN 333 research programme](https://medical.biomarin.com/en-us/bmn-333/)

**Gene editing and regenerative medicine.** The most genuinely curative concept is correction or silencing of the pathogenic FGFR3 allele in growth-plate cells. In a 2021 preclinical study, researchers reprogrammed cells from people with achondroplasia into induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs), corrected the FGFR3 mutation with CRISPR–Cas9 in culture, and restored cartilage-cell differentiation in the corrected cells. [Targeted gene correction in patient-derived iPSCs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8403427/) This is important proof of principle, but it remains a laboratory result: a clinical cure would require safe, efficient delivery to the many cartilage cells of a growing skeleton, durable correction, and rigorous assessment of unintended edits and developmental effects.

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

The strongest completed evidence since 2020 is for growth-modifying medicines. Vosoritide’s phase 3 trial supported its approval, while its open-label extension showed that the growth-velocity effect persisted through two years of treatment. [Vosoritide phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32891212/) [Vosoritide two-year extension](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34341520/) Navepegritide’s phase 2 programme supported its 2026 accelerated approval, but the FDA requires a long-term externally controlled study measuring final adult height, disproportionality, neurological outcomes, bone deformity, bone age, sleep apnea, and safety; its planned trial completion is September 2034. [FDA approval letter for navepegritide](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/219164Orig1s000ltr.pdf)

BridgeBio/QED’s infigratinib programme has moved from a phase 2 dose-finding study in children aged 3–11 years to the phase 3 PROPEL 3 study in children aged 3–17 years. [Infigratinib phase 2 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555818/) [Infigratinib phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42370681/) The phase 3 results were positive for the growth-velocity endpoint, but infigratinib was still an investigational achondroplasia therapy as of August 8, 2026, rather than an approved cure or established long-term standard. [Infigratinib phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42370681/)

Not every biologically plausible approach has succeeded. Pfizer’s soluble decoy-receptor approach, **recifercept**, was intended to bind fibroblast growth factors before they activated FGFR3. In an open-label phase 2 study of 57 treated children, the sponsor stopped the trial early because the observed growth increase did not exceed that expected from untreated external-control data; the study was not stopped for safety concerns. [Recifercept phase 2 study results](https://www.pfizer.com/sites/default/files/plsr-studies/C4181005%20Plain%20Language%20Study%20Results%20Summary%20%28PLSRS%29%20Phase%202-4.pdf)

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Achondroplasia researchers combine genetically engineered mouse models, patient-derived cells, natural-history registries, imaging, and clinical growth studies. Mouse experiments allow investigators to test whether treatment normalizes growth-plate structure and long-bone growth; for example, low-dose infigratinib improved bone growth and growth-plate abnormalities in an achondroplasia mouse model. [Infigratinib mouse-model study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38590263/) Patient-derived iPSCs permit direct testing of cartilage differentiation and genome-editing strategies in human cells carrying the relevant mutation. [Targeted gene correction in patient-derived iPSCs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8403427/)

Clinical studies use annualized growth velocity, achondroplasia-specific height z scores, body proportions, bone age, radiographs, safety monitoring, and biomarkers of growth-plate activity. The infigratinib programme, for example, includes pharmacokinetics and collagen-X-related pharmacodynamic measures alongside detailed limb and trunk measurements. [Infigratinib trial registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04265651) Natural-history datasets are also essential because they provide untreated reference data and clarify complication rates; the U.S. CLARITY registry spans four skeletal-dysplasia centers and included 1,374 people in a recent hydrocephalus analysis. [CLARITY natural-history study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37877951/)

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

A major strength of the field is unusually clear biology: achondroplasia is most often caused by the same activating FGFR3 variant, and several therapies now target either that pathway directly or its downstream signaling. [International consensus statement](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41574-021-00595-x) The transition from no approved growth-directed drug before 2021 to daily and weekly approved CNP-pathway medicines, plus positive oral-FGFR-inhibitor trial results, is a meaningful therapeutic advance. [FDA approval of vosoritide](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-improve-growth-children-most-common-form-dwarfism) [FDA approval letter for navepegritide](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/219164Orig1s000ltr.pdf) [Infigratinib phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42370681/)

The central limitation is that greater childhood growth is not synonymous with cure or with prevention of every complication. Evidence for final adult height, spinal and neurologic outcomes, sleep-disordered breathing, quality of life, and long-term safety remains incomplete; both FDA accelerated approvals require post-marketing evaluation of adult height and clinically important outcomes. [FDA approval of vosoritide](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-improve-growth-children-most-common-form-dwarfism) [FDA approval letter for navepegritide](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/219164Orig1s000ltr.pdf) Direct FGFR inhibition also raises the need for especially careful long-term monitoring because FGFR signaling has normal roles beyond the growth plate, while CRISPR-based correction faces the still-unsolved challenge of delivering editing tools safely and broadly enough to developing skeletal tissues. [Infigratinib phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42370681/) [Targeted gene correction in patient-derived iPSCs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8403427/)

## Outlook and Future Directions

As of August 8, 2026, achondroplasia has entered an era of targeted disease modification, but not cure. The most important milestones to watch are long-term adult-height and complication data for vosoritide and navepegritide, regulatory decisions and extended safety follow-up for oral infigratinib, comparative results from the BMN 333-versus-vosoritide trial, and evidence that gene-editing or allele-silencing platforms can reach growth-plate cartilage safely in vivo. [FDA approval letter for navepegritide](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/appletter/2026/219164Orig1s000ltr.pdf) [Infigratinib phase 3 trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42370681/) [ASPEN trial registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07441876) A true cure will require permanent, safe correction of the pathogenic FGFR3 signal early enough to alter skeletal development, a threshold that current clinical medicines and laboratory-stage gene editing have not yet reached. [Targeted gene correction in patient-derived iPSCs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8403427/)
