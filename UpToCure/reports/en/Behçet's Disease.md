# Behçet's Disease

## Overview

Behçet's disease is a chronic, relapsing inflammatory disorder in which the immune system causes vasculitis—harmful inflammation of blood vessels—throughout the body. Typical features include recurrent painful mouth and genital ulcers, skin lesions, joint inflammation, and eye inflammation (uveitis); some people also develop blood clots, gastrointestinal ulcers, or inflammation affecting the brain and spinal cord. It is uncommon in the United States, more prevalent across parts of the Middle East and Asia, and most often begins in a person’s 20s or 30s, although it can occur at any age. [MedlinePlus overview](https://medlineplus.gov/behcetssyndrome.html) [MedlinePlus Genetics](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/behcet-disease/)

The course varies widely. Some people have predominantly mucosal ulcers and intermittent joint symptoms, whereas eye, neurologic, vascular, or intestinal disease can cause irreversible organ damage. There is currently no cure; standard care suppresses inflammation and prevents damage using symptom-directed topical treatment, colchicine, short courses of corticosteroids, conventional immune-suppressing drugs such as azathioprine, and targeted biologics such as anti-tumor necrosis factor (anti-TNF) medicines for serious or refractory disease. In the United States, apremilast is FDA-approved specifically for oral ulcers in adults with Behçet's disease. [British 2024 living guideline](https://academic.oup.com/bjd/article/191/5/e8/7754287) [FDA apremilast approval record](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=384812)

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Research since 2020 has been active but remains focused on achieving durable, steroid-sparing remission rather than eliminating the underlying disease permanently. The major questions are which immune pathway should be targeted for each clinical phenotype—especially severe eye, blood-vessel, neurologic, and intestinal involvement—and whether genetic, transcriptomic, metabolomic, or cell-level biomarkers can match an individual to the right treatment. The field is not close to a broadly applicable curative therapy: Behçet's is biologically heterogeneous and usually polygenic, so current clinical development is centered on better immune control, not gene replacement, gene editing, cell therapy, or RNA therapies. ## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

The strongest recent therapeutic advance is better evidence for targeted TNF blockade in severe disease. In a multicenter phase 2 randomized trial of 52 people with major vascular or central-nervous-system Behçet's, infliximab plus the same corticosteroid regimen produced complete response at week 22 in 81% of participants, compared with 56% receiving cyclophosphamide; overall adverse events were also less frequent with infliximab. This is an important improvement in induction treatment for high-risk disease, but it demonstrates control of active inflammation, not a lasting cure after treatment withdrawal. [Infliximab versus cyclophosphamide trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39437137/)

A separate United Kingdom randomized study, BIO BEHÇET'S, directly compared infliximab with interferon-alpha in 79 people with refractory active disease. Both biologics improved disease activity to a similar extent at 12 and 24 weeks, with a nonsignificant trend toward better tolerability and treatment persistence for infliximab. The trial also explored pharmacogenetics and metabolomics—measurement of small molecules in blood and urine—to identify predictors of response, an early step toward personalized treatment rather than one-size-fits-all immune suppression. [BIO BEHÇET'S randomized trial report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39504391/)

Small-molecule immune modulators remain an important practical strategy. Apremilast, an oral phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitor, is approved in the United States for Behçet-related oral ulcers and is useful for a specific symptom domain, not for preventing all forms of organ-threatening disease. A newer experimental direction is Janus kinase (JAK) inhibition, which blocks intracellular signaling used by several inflammatory cytokines. As of August 8, 2026, a phase 2 multicenter study of the selective JAK inhibitor ivarmacitinib for refractory Behçet's had been registered by Peking University People's Hospital, but was listed as not yet recruiting and had no outcomes available. [FDA apremilast prescribing information](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/205437s013lbl.pdf) [Ivarmacitinib phase 2 study](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07634848)

At the discovery level, researchers are mapping the immune circuitry that might yield more precise treatments. A 2023 single-cell multi-omics study profiled 424,817 blood cells and found cell-specific inflammatory programs involving transcription-factor families including AP-1, NF-kB, and ETS; these are regulators that switch suites of immune genes on and off. Genetic sequencing is also increasingly used in people with very early-onset, familial, or atypical Behçet-like illness to identify monogenic immune disorders that mimic Behçet's, such as haploinsufficiency of A20. This can alter diagnosis and treatment, but it is not evidence that gene therapy is being developed for typical Behçet's disease. [Single-cell multi-omics study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37848613/) [British 2024 living guideline](https://academic.oup.com/bjd/article/191/5/e8/7754287)

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

Recent completed trials have strengthened the case for biologic treatment in refractory and severe disease. The phase 2 infliximab-versus-cyclophosphamide study was funded by the French Ministry of Health and favored infliximab for complete response at 22 weeks in severe vascular or neurologic Behçet's. BIO BEHÇET'S, funded by the United Kingdom National Institute for Health and Care Research (NIHR), was the first prospective head-to-head randomized comparison of infliximab and interferon-alpha in refractory disease and found comparable efficacy. [Infliximab versus cyclophosphamide trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39437137/) [BIO BEHÇET'S randomized trial report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39504391/)

Several experimental studies remain in progress or planning stages. The UVB phase 2 trial is designed to compare adalimumab with tocilizumab for severe, sight-threatening Behçet uveitis, with an estimated enrollment of 60 and primary completion projected for November 2026. A Chinese recruiting study is evaluating lenalidomide in 42 people with refractory mucosal Behçet's. These trials may improve selection among immune-modulating drugs, but neither is designed to establish a permanent cure. [Adalimumab versus tocilizumab UVB trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05874505) [Lenalidomide mucosal Behçet trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05449548)

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Researchers combine conventional randomized controlled trials with deep immune profiling. Clinical studies increasingly measure organ-specific activity, steroid reduction, quality of life, imaging findings, and safety, because Behçet's can affect different organs in different people and standard blood inflammation markers may not reliably reflect a flare. Contemporary guidelines prioritize remission induction, maintenance of remission, and prevention of blindness, neurologic injury, thrombosis, and intestinal complications rather than a single universal activity score. [British 2024 living guideline](https://academic.oup.com/bjd/article/191/5/e8/7754287)

Mechanistic studies use next-generation sequencing, blood transcriptomics, single-cell RNA sequencing, single-cell chromatin-accessibility mapping, and metabolomics. These platforms can identify which immune-cell populations and inflammatory gene networks are active in particular disease phenotypes, then test whether a targeted drug changes those signatures. The approach is promising for biomarker-guided trials, but findings from blood cells must still be validated in larger, diverse cohorts and linked to meaningful clinical outcomes. [Single-cell multi-omics study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37848613/) [BIO BEHÇET'S randomized trial report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39504391/)

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

A central strength of recent work is the move from uncontrolled case series toward comparative trials in difficult, organ-threatening Behçet's. Infliximab’s favorable phase 2 results against cyclophosphamide and the direct comparison of infliximab with interferon-alpha give clinicians stronger evidence for choosing treatment than was available previously. Single-cell and multi-omics studies also create a plausible route to classify biologically distinct subgroups rather than treating all Behçet's as one disease. [Infliximab versus cyclophosphamide trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39437137/) [BIO BEHÇET'S randomized trial report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39504391/) [Single-cell multi-omics study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37848613/)

The limitations are substantial. Trials are necessarily small because Behçet's is rare and clinically diverse; many drugs are used off-label; severe manifestations lack high-certainty comparative evidence; and immunosuppression can bring infection, toxicity, cost, and access barriers. A treatment that works for oral ulcers may not prevent blindness, thrombosis, or neurologic injury. Moreover, even successful biologic therapy generally requires ongoing administration, so clinical remission should not be confused with immune reset or cure. [British 2024 living guideline](https://academic.oup.com/bjd/article/191/5/e8/7754287) [FDA apremilast prescribing information](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/205437s013lbl.pdf)

## Outlook and Future Directions

As of August 8, 2026, a cure for typical Behçet's disease is not imminent. The most realistic near-term milestones are confirmation that targeted agents such as JAK inhibitors or IL-6 blockade can safely improve refractory disease, completion of the adalimumab-versus-tocilizumab uveitis trial, and validation of genetic, cellular, and metabolic biomarkers that predict response before irreversible organ damage occurs. A true cure would likely require a much clearer explanation of disease initiation and persistence, followed by a durable and safe way to restore immune tolerance rather than simply suppress inflammatory pathways.
