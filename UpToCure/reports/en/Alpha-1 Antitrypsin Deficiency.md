# Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency

## Overview

Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency (AATD) is an inherited condition caused by disease-causing variants in the *SERPINA1* gene, which directs the liver to make alpha-1 antitrypsin (AAT). AAT normally protects lung tissue from neutrophil elastase, an immune-system enzyme that can damage the lungs if not adequately controlled. In the common severe Pi*ZZ form, the altered “Z” AAT protein is both scarce in the bloodstream and prone to misfolding and accumulating in liver cells; this creates a dual problem of early emphysema or other chronic obstructive lung disease and potential liver inflammation, fibrosis, cirrhosis, or liver cancer. Disease severity varies substantially by genotype, smoking and other exposures, and individual biology; lung disease often emerges in adulthood, particularly among people who smoke, while liver disease can occur from infancy through later life. [GeneReviews overview](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK1519/) ([ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK1519/?utm_source=openai))

There is no approved molecular cure. Current care emphasizes avoiding smoking and lung irritants, standard chronic obstructive pulmonary disease treatments, vaccinations, monitoring of lung and liver health, and—in selected adults with AATD-related emphysema—regular intravenous infusion of plasma-derived AAT (“augmentation therapy”). Augmentation replaces missing protein but does not repair *SERPINA1*, remove toxic Z-AAT from the liver, or treat AATD liver disease; advanced liver disease may require transplantation. [Clinical practice guideline](https://journal.copdfoundation.org/jcopdf/id/1115/The-Diagnosis-and-Management-of-Alpha-1-Antitrypsin-Deficiency-in-the-Adult) ([journal.copdfoundation.org](https://journal.copdfoundation.org/jcopdf/id/1115/The-Diagnosis-and-Management-of-Alpha-1-Antitrypsin-Deficiency-in-the-Adult?utm_source=openai))

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Research activity since 2020 has been unusually strong because AATD has a well-defined genetic target and because a true cure would ideally solve both components of the disease: restore normal circulating AAT for the lungs and stop mutant-protein accumulation in the liver. The field has progressed from cell and mouse correction experiments to early human testing of in vivo base editing, while RNA interference has advanced furthest for AATD-related liver disease. Nevertheless, only BEAM-302 is presently attempting a one-time direct correction of the common PiZ mutation in people; its early results are promising but remain insufficient to establish a durable cure. [BEAM-302 trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06389877) ([clinicaltrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06389877?utm_source=openai))

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

**In vivo gene editing is the leading potentially curative strategy.** The PiZ mutation is a single DNA-letter change, making it an attractive target for base editing, a gene-editing approach that can alter one DNA base without creating the double-strand DNA breaks used in conventional CRISPR cutting. In 2022, researchers delivered base-editor messenger RNA and guide RNA in lipid nanoparticles to PiZ-transgenic mice, producing durable liver editing, higher circulating AAT, and improved liver histology. [Base editing in PiZ mice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35121111/) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35121111/?utm_source=openai))

The key clinical advance is Beam Therapeutics’ BEAM-302, a liver-targeted lipid-nanoparticle therapy designed to convert the PiZ sequence back to the normal PiM sequence in hepatocytes. In company-reported Phase 1/2 data current through February 10, 2026, 29 participants had received treatment; among recipients of a single 60-mg dose, mean steady-state total AAT was reported as 16.1 micromolar, with all participants above the 11-micromolar protective threshold for up to 12 months, corrected M-AAT comprising 94% of total AAT, and an 84% reduction in mutant Z-AAT. These findings are important because they suggest one intervention may restore functional AAT while also reducing the liver-toxic protein, but they are preliminary company data rather than a peer-reviewed efficacy demonstration. [BEAM-302 March 2026 update](https://investors.beamtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/beam-therapeutics-announces-compelling-updated-clinical-data) ([investors.beamtx.com](https://investors.beamtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/beam-therapeutics-announces-compelling-updated-clinical-data?utm_source=openai))

**RNA interference is the most clinically advanced liver-directed disease-modifying approach, but not a genetic cure.** Fazirsiran, formerly ARO-AAT and now also known as TAK-999, is a small interfering RNA designed to reduce production of Z-AAT messenger RNA in the liver. In an open-label Phase 2 study in Pi*ZZ adults with liver fibrosis, fazirsiran substantially lowered liver Z-AAT and reduced abnormal Z-AAT-containing globules on liver biopsy; the study also documented why lung monitoring is essential, because suppressing already-low circulating AAT could theoretically worsen emphysema. [Phase 2 fazirsiran study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2205416) ([nejm.org](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2205416?utm_source=openai)) A subsequent placebo-controlled Phase 2 study, SEQUOIA, randomized 40 people and tested whether lowering Z-AAT translated into improvement in liver injury and fibrosis measures. [SEQUOIA Phase 2 trial](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016508524051813) ([sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016508524051813?utm_source=openai))

**Protein correctors and replacement technologies offer useful lessons but are not cures.** Small-molecule “correctors” aim to help Z-AAT fold and leave liver cells normally. Vertex’s VX-864 achieved statistically significant increases in functional AAT in a Phase 2 study, but the increase was considered too small to be likely to yield substantial clinical benefit, so the program did not proceed to late-stage development. [VX-864 Phase 2 results](https://investors.vrtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/vertex-announces-primary-endpoint-achieved-phase-2-study-vx-864) ([investors.vrtx.com](https://investors.vrtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/vertex-announces-primary-endpoint-achieved-phase-2-study-vx-864?utm_source=openai)) Long-acting recombinant AAT replacement candidates, including INBRX-101/SAR447537, aim to reduce the infusion burden of augmentation therapy; they may improve protein replacement but do not correct the inherited mutation or eliminate hepatic Z-AAT accumulation. [INBRX-101 Phase 1 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03815396) ([clinicaltrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03815396?rank=1&term=Inhibrx&viewType=Table&utm_source=openai))

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

BEAM-302 is an ongoing, recruiting, multicenter Phase 1/2 study sponsored by Beam Therapeutics in adults with AATD-associated lung disease and/or liver disease. The study began on June 19, 2024, has an estimated enrollment of 106, and is designed to establish safety, dose, biological activity, and clinical effects of a one-time base-editing infusion. Beam has selected 60 mg for planned pivotal development and stated that it expects to begin a pivotal cohort in the second half of 2026; that plan and any potential accelerated-approval pathway remain prospective. [BEAM-302 clinical trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06389877) ([clinicaltrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06389877?utm_source=openai)) [BEAM-302 development update](https://investors.beamtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/beam-therapeutics-announces-compelling-updated-clinical-data) ([investors.beamtx.com](https://investors.beamtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/beam-therapeutics-announces-compelling-updated-clinical-data?utm_source=openai))

Fazirsiran remains the principal late-stage liver-directed RNA therapy. Takeda sponsors a recruiting Phase 3 placebo-controlled study in people with AATD liver disease and mild fibrosis, with liver-biopsy endpoints and estimated primary completion in August 2028. Fazirsiran could become an important treatment for liver disease if efficacy and lung safety are confirmed, but because it requires repeated dosing and reduces expression rather than repairing *SERPINA1*, it should be considered disease-modifying rather than curative. [Fazirsiran Phase 3 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06165341) ([clinicaltrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06165341?intr=fazirsiran&rank=3&utm_source=openai))

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Researchers use complementary models to test whether therapies can address the lung-and-liver biology of AATD. Patient-derived induced pluripotent stem cells—adult cells reprogrammed into stem-like cells—and hepatocyte-like cells allow investigators to correct the PiZ mutation in human genetic backgrounds and then measure AAT secretion, intracellular protein accumulation, endoplasmic-reticulum stress, and unintended DNA changes. A 2021 study found that adenine base editing of patient-derived cells reduced abnormal AAT accumulation, increased secretion, and did not identify off-target genomic mutations by whole-genome sequencing in the tested cell lines. [Patient iPSC base-editing study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8571173/) ([pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8571173/?utm_source=openai))

Animal studies are used to evaluate delivery, tissue editing, durability, toxicity, and the effect on liver injury before human trials. Lipid nanoparticles are particularly attractive because they naturally concentrate in the liver after intravenous administration and can transiently deliver editor messenger RNA plus a guide RNA. Clinical studies then follow blood total and functional AAT, the proportion of normal M-AAT versus mutant Z-AAT, liver enzymes, elastase-inhibition activity, lung function, imaging, and—when appropriate—liver-biopsy measures such as Z-AAT burden and METAVIR fibrosis stage. [Base editing in PiZ mice](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35121111/) ([pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35121111/?utm_source=openai)) [SEQUOIA Phase 2 trial](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016508524051813) ([sciencedirect.com](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0016508524051813?utm_source=openai))

## Leading Institutions and Funding

The field combines academic rare-disease centers with industry programs. Beam Therapeutics leads the clinical base-editing program; Takeda and Arrowhead Pharmaceuticals have advanced fazirsiran; and centers in the United States and Europe have contributed liver-biopsy studies, pulmonary trials, patient registries, and translational models. The Alpha-1 Foundation is a central nonprofit funder and infrastructure provider, reporting more than $92 million invested in Alpha-1 research and programs across 129 institutions by 2023; it also supports grant programs, a therapeutic-development network, tissue resources, and a research registry. ## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The strongest scientific case for a cure is direct correction of PiZ in liver cells: one edit could potentially allow normal physiological AAT production, prevent ongoing mutant-protein accumulation, and address both lung and liver mechanisms at once. The early BEAM-302 biomarker data are therefore a major milestone, especially because they include sustained production of corrected M-AAT and large reductions in Z-AAT after one dose. However, clinical benefit—slower emphysema progression, reduced liver fibrosis progression, fewer transplants, and improved survival—has not yet been demonstrated, and the current data set is small with limited follow-up. [BEAM-302 March 2026 update](https://investors.beamtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/beam-therapeutics-announces-compelling-updated-clinical-data) ([investors.beamtx.com](https://investors.beamtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/beam-therapeutics-announces-compelling-updated-clinical-data?utm_source=openai))

Important unresolved issues include long-term editing durability, rare off-target edits, immune or infusion reactions, the fraction of hepatocytes that must be corrected for lasting benefit, treatment of people with advanced cirrhosis, and whether corrected liver output will prevent or reverse established lung damage. RNA interference has robust evidence of lowering hepatic Z-AAT, but it is repeat-dose therapy and may leave patients with too little protective circulating AAT unless lung risk is carefully managed. Access and affordability will also matter: complex one-time genetic medicines may be difficult to deliver equitably even if their benefits are confirmed. [Phase 2 fazirsiran study](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2205416) ([nejm.org](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2205416?utm_source=openai))

## Outlook and Future Directions

As of August 8, 2026, AATD is closer to a plausible genetic cure than it was in 2020, chiefly because in vivo base editing has now produced early human evidence of PiZ correction and therapeutic AAT restoration. It is still too early to call BEAM-302 curative: the milestones to watch are the planned pivotal cohort, independently scrutinized longer-term safety and durability data, confirmation of lung and liver clinical outcomes rather than biomarkers alone, and eventual regulatory review. In parallel, Phase 3 fazirsiran results will clarify whether sustained Z-AAT suppression can delay liver disease while preserving pulmonary safety.
