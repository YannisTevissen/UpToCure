# Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency

## Overview

Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency, often called AATD or “Alpha-1,” is an inherited condition caused by variants in the SERPINA1 gene, which normally instructs liver cells to make alpha-1 antitrypsin, a protective protein that helps shield the lungs from inflammation-related damage [MedlinePlus Genetics](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/alpha-1-antitrypsin-deficiency/). The common severe PiZ form causes two problems at once: too little working alpha-1 antitrypsin reaches the blood and lungs, raising the risk of emphysema and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, while misfolded Z-alpha-1 antitrypsin can accumulate inside liver cells and cause fibrosis, cirrhosis, or liver cancer [2024 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11601085/).

AATD affects children and adults, but lung disease is strongly influenced by smoking and environmental exposures, and liver disease can appear from infancy through later adulthood [MedlinePlus Genetics](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/alpha-1-antitrypsin-deficiency/). Current care is not curative and focuses on smoking avoidance, vaccination, standard COPD inhalers and pulmonary rehabilitation when needed, monitoring for liver disease, intravenous plasma-derived alpha-1 antitrypsin augmentation for selected adults with airflow obstruction, and lung or liver transplantation for end-stage disease [COPD Foundation guideline](https://journal.copdfoundation.org/jcopdf/id/1115/The-Diagnosis-and-Management-of-Alpha-1-Antitrypsin-Deficiency-in-the-Adult).

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

Since 2020, AATD research has shifted from treating downstream lung inflammation toward disease-modifying and potentially curative strategies that either silence toxic Z-alpha-1 antitrypsin production in the liver, repair or edit SERPINA1 RNA or DNA, add a working SERPINA1 gene, or improve protein replacement delivery and durability [2024 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11601085/). The field is active and clinically advanced for RNA interference and replacement approaches, newly clinical for RNA editing and DNA base editing, and still early for true one-time curative gene correction, so a broadly available cure is not yet in hand [BEAM-302 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06165783).

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

RNA interference is the most clinically mature liver-directed strategy, and its goal is to lower production of the misfolded Z protein that injures hepatocytes rather than to raise lung-protective alpha-1 antitrypsin [fazirsiran phase 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36342149/). In a phase 2 open-label study of fazirsiran in adults with PiZZ AATD and liver fibrosis, serum Z-alpha-1 antitrypsin fell by a median of 83%, total liver Z-alpha-1 antitrypsin fell by a median of 94%, liver globule burden improved in all paired biopsies, fibrosis regressed in 7 of 12 paired-biopsy participants and progressed in 3 of 12, and no serious adverse events or clinically meaningful pulmonary-function declines were reported [fazirsiran phase 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36342149/). Belcesiran, another subcutaneous RNA interference therapy, is being tested by Novo Nordisk in adult PiZZ participants with AATD-associated liver disease to measure safety, pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics, and alpha-1 antitrypsin lowering [belcesiran trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05218083/).

RNA editing is a newer strategy that tries to convert the mutant PiZ RNA message into a message that can produce healthy M-alpha-1 antitrypsin without permanently changing DNA [WVE-006 explainer](https://alpha1.org/wve-006-aimer/). WVE-006 is a GalNAc-conjugated oligonucleotide designed to recruit endogenous ADAR RNA-editing enzymes in hepatocytes, and 2025 nonclinical data showed up to 50% RNA editing and 60% wild-type M-alpha-1 antitrypsin among total alpha-1 antitrypsin in primary human PiZZ hepatocytes, durable editing for 7 weeks after one dose in PiZ mice, and no liver off-target RNA editing detected in mice or nonhuman primates [RNA-editing discovery](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40017292/). A first-in-human phase 1b/2a RestorAATion-2 trial is evaluating WVE-006 in PiZZ patients with AATD, with ClinicalTrials.gov listing 36 participants and submitted results as of its 2025 update [WVE-006 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06186492).

DNA gene editing aims to correct the root SERPINA1 mutation more permanently, which makes it potentially curative but also raises higher safety standards for off-target editing, liver delivery, durability, and reversibility [BEAM-302 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06165783). BEAM-302 is a biological base-editing therapy intended to correct the disease-causing G-to-A point mutation associated with PiZZ AATD, and its phase 1/2 open-label dose-escalation and dose-expansion trial is enrolling adults with the PiZZ genotype [BEAM-302 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06165783). Preclinical academic work has also shown that CRISPR homology-directed repair and base-editing approaches can correct the Z mutation in patient-derived liver organoids, induced pluripotent stem cell-derived hepatocyte-like cells, and engineered SERPINA1 models, supporting the feasibility of gene correction while underscoring delivery and manufacturing challenges [SERPINA1 gene-editing study](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/21/16/5702).

Gene addition and improved protein replacement remain important lung-directed strategies because lowering mutant Z protein in the liver does not automatically restore enough circulating alpha-1 antitrypsin to protect the lungs [2024 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11601085/). APB-101 is a recombinant AAVrh.10 human alpha-1 antitrypsin vector being studied in a small phase 1/2 trial sponsored by the University of Massachusetts Worcester with Applied Genetic Technologies Corp and NHLBI listed as collaborators [APB-101 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04563975). INBRX-101 is a biologic replacement candidate being compared with Zemaira in a phase 2 randomized trial in adults with AATD emphysema, and Kamada-AAT for Inhalation is being tested as an 80 mg daily inhaled therapy in a phase 3 trial for patients with AATD [INBRX-101 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05856331), [Kamada inhaled AAT trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04204252).

Cell therapy is less advanced clinically but is important as a research platform and possible future regenerative option for liver disease [iPSC-hepatocyte modeling study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38285331/). In 2024, single-cell RNA sequencing of autologous AATD induced pluripotent stem cell-derived hepatocytes showed unfolded-protein-response, endoplasmic-reticulum protein-processing, and apoptosis signatures compared with healthy controls, supporting these cells as disease models and drug-discovery tools [iPSC-hepatocyte modeling study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38285331/).

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

The most advanced liver-disease program is fazirsiran, which has moved from phase 2 biopsy-based evidence into a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial sponsored by Takeda in adults with AATD-associated liver disease and METAVIR F2 to F4 fibrosis [fazirsiran phase 3 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05677971). The phase 3 trial plans about 160 participants, treats for two years with six months of follow-up, and tests whether fazirsiran reduces liver fibrosis compared with placebo after 106 weeks [fazirsiran phase 3 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05677971). Belcesiran is in a phase 2 placebo-controlled trial of about 78 adult PiZZ participants with AATD-associated liver disease, with safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetic, and pharmacodynamic endpoints [belcesiran trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05218083/).

The first human RNA-editing and DNA base-editing studies are smaller early-phase trials designed primarily to establish safety, dose, pharmacodynamics, and proof of biological effect [WVE-006 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06186492), [BEAM-302 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06165783). RestorAATion-2 is an open-label phase 1b/2a WVE-006 study in PiZZ AATD, and BEAM-302 is an open-label phase 1/2 study of a base-editing biological therapy in PiZZ AATD [WVE-006 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06186492), [BEAM-302 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06165783).

For lung-directed approaches, APB-101 is a very small phase 1/2 AAV gene-therapy trial with an estimated enrollment of five adults, INBRX-101 is a phase 2 randomized trial with an estimated enrollment of 90 adults with AATD emphysema, and the Kamada inhaled alpha-1 antitrypsin phase 3 study enrolled 202 participants and had no results posted on ClinicalTrials.gov as of its 2026 update [APB-101 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04563975), [INBRX-101 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05856331), [Kamada inhaled AAT trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04204252).

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Researchers are combining liver biopsies, fibrosis staging, serum and intrahepatic Z-alpha-1 antitrypsin measurements, liver enzymes, pulmonary-function testing, and safety monitoring to determine whether experimental therapies actually change the biology of AATD rather than only symptoms [fazirsiran phase 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36342149/). Delivery platforms include subcutaneous GalNAc-conjugated oligonucleotides for hepatocyte targeting, adeno-associated viral vectors for gene addition, biologic protein replacement, inhaled alpha-1 antitrypsin for local lung delivery, and lipid-nanoparticle or comparable liver-targeted systems for editing programs [RNA-editing discovery](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40017292/), [APB-101 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04563975), [Kamada inhaled AAT trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04204252).

Model systems have become more human-relevant since 2020, with patient-derived liver organoids, induced pluripotent stem cell-derived hepatocyte-like cells, primary human PiZZ hepatocytes, PiZ transgenic mice, and nonhuman primates used to test editing efficiency, secretion of functional M-alpha-1 antitrypsin, liver toxicity, and off-target effects [SERPINA1 gene-editing study](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/21/16/5702), [RNA-editing discovery](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40017292/). Single-cell RNA sequencing is also being used to characterize AATD hepatocyte stress pathways and to improve disease models for drug discovery [iPSC-hepatocyte modeling study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38285331/).

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The field’s main strength is that AATD has a well-defined genetic cause, measurable circulating protein, accessible liver biomarkers, and a clear toxic liver protein target, which makes it unusually suitable for RNA silencing, RNA editing, and DNA editing [MedlinePlus Genetics](https://medlineplus.gov/genetics/condition/alpha-1-antitrypsin-deficiency/), [fazirsiran phase 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36342149/). The strongest clinical signal so far is liver-directed RNA interference with fazirsiran, but this approach may worsen or fail to correct the lung-protection problem unless paired with augmentation or a therapy that restores functional alpha-1 antitrypsin [fazirsiran phase 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36342149/), [COPD Foundation guideline](https://journal.copdfoundation.org/jcopdf/id/1115/The-Diagnosis-and-Management-of-Alpha-1-Antitrypsin-Deficiency-in-the-Adult).

The biggest unresolved issues are whether liver histology improvements translate into fewer cases of cirrhosis and liver cancer, whether editing can achieve enough durable M-alpha-1 antitrypsin to protect lungs without dangerous off-target effects, whether AAV gene therapy can be redosed or scaled safely, and whether costly advanced therapies can reach the many underdiagnosed patients who currently receive only supportive care [fazirsiran phase 3 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05677971), [BEAM-302 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06165783), [APB-101 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04563975). Access is also a practical challenge because current augmentation therapy already requires lifelong infusions for selected patients, and next-generation biologics, oligonucleotides, and editing therapies may require specialized monitoring and payer support [COPD Foundation guideline](https://journal.copdfoundation.org/jcopdf/id/1115/The-Diagnosis-and-Management-of-Alpha-1-Antitrypsin-Deficiency-in-the-Adult).

## Outlook and Future Directions

A cure for AATD is plausible but not yet proven, and the next decisive milestones are phase 3 fazirsiran fibrosis outcomes, published human WVE-006 RNA-editing results, early BEAM-302 safety and editing data, APB-101 gene-expression results, and whether lung-directed replacement programs can reduce emphysema progression more conveniently than current weekly intravenous augmentation [fazirsiran phase 3 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05677971), [WVE-006 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06186492), [BEAM-302 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06165783), [APB-101 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04563975), [INBRX-101 trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05856331). The most likely near-term future is not a single universal cure but a split strategy: RNA silencing for liver-dominant disease, augmentation or inhaled/recombinant replacement for lung protection, and eventually RNA or DNA editing for patients in whom one treatment can both reduce toxic Z protein and restore functional M-alpha-1 antitrypsin [RNA-editing discovery](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40017292/).
