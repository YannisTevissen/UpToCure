# Alagille Syndrome

## Overview

Alagille Syndrome is a rare genetic condition that can affect the liver, heart, eyes, skeleton, kidneys, blood vessels, and growth; most diagnosed cases are caused by a disease-causing variant in one copy of JAG1, and a smaller proportion are caused by NOTCH2, both of which are genes in the Notch signaling pathway that helps organs form before birth [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/). In the liver, the central problem is bile duct paucity, meaning too few small bile ducts, which can lead to cholestasis, or poor bile flow, causing jaundice, severe itching, poor growth, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency, xanthomas, and progressive liver disease in some children [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/).

There is no established cure that corrects the underlying JAG1 or NOTCH2 defect, so current care is supportive and organ-specific: nutrition and vitamin replacement, monitoring of heart and kidney disease, treatment of cholestatic itching, and liver transplantation for liver failure or severe liver-driven symptoms that cannot be controlled medically [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/). Since 2021, the most important therapeutic change has been the approval of ileal bile acid transporter inhibitors, or IBAT inhibitors, which reduce intestinal reabsorption of bile acids and can improve cholestatic pruritus, including maralixibat/Livmarli and odevixibat/Bylvay [FDA Livmarli approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-treat-itching-children-rare-liver-disease) [FDA odevixibat approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-odevixibat-cholestatic-pruritus-patients-alagille-syndrome) [EMA Livmarli EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/livmarli) [EMA Bylvay EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/bylvay).

## Scope of Recent Research (2020–present)

From 2020 to today, Alagille Syndrome research has been clinically active but not yet curative: the strongest progress has come from drug trials targeting cholestasis and itching, while disease-modifying ideas such as gene replacement, gene editing, RNA-based therapy, and regenerative repair remain preclinical or conceptual for this disorder [ClinicalTrials.gov Alagille studies](https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?cond=Alagille%20Syndrome) [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/). The main research questions are how to improve quality of life, reduce bile-acid toxicity, identify which children will progress to transplant, and eventually determine whether safe restoration of JAG1/NOTCH2-Notch signaling can preserve or rebuild bile ducts [ClinicalTrials.gov Alagille studies](https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?cond=Alagille%20Syndrome) [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/).

## Major Breakthroughs and Emerging Therapies

The major recent breakthrough is the IBAT-inhibitor class. Maralixibat became the first FDA-approved medicine for cholestatic pruritus in children with Alagille Syndrome, and FDA described trial evidence showing lower itching scores compared with placebo, with common adverse effects including diarrhea and abdominal pain [FDA Livmarli approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-treat-itching-children-rare-liver-disease). The peer-reviewed ICONIC study evaluated maralixibat in children with Alagille Syndrome and reported durable reductions in pruritus and serum bile acids in responders, supporting the concept that interrupting bile-acid recirculation can meaningfully improve symptoms even without correcting the genetic cause [ICONIC maralixibat publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34748612/) [ICONIC trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02160782).

Odevixibat is the second important IBAT-inhibitor advance. The ASSERT study is registered as a phase 3 randomized trial in patients with Alagille Syndrome, and FDA approved odevixibat for cholestatic pruritus in patients with Alagille Syndrome after review of clinical data [ASSERT trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04674761) [FDA odevixibat approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-odevixibat-cholestatic-pruritus-patients-alagille-syndrome). EMA public assessment pages for Livmarli and Bylvay show that European regulators have also reviewed these IBAT inhibitors for rare pediatric cholestatic disease, giving clinicians more than one approved oral option for itch control [EMA Livmarli EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/livmarli) [EMA Bylvay EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/bylvay).

Gene therapy and gene editing are the most direct curative ideas because Alagille Syndrome is usually caused by insufficient function of one JAG1 copy, or less often NOTCH2 [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/). However, no registered clinical trial is testing a JAG1 or NOTCH2 gene-replacement or gene-editing cure in patients as of May 2026, and the biology is difficult because Notch signaling is involved in embryonic development, blood vessels, the heart, kidneys, and other organs, not only the liver [ClinicalTrials.gov Alagille studies](https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?cond=Alagille%20Syndrome) [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/).

Cell and regenerative approaches are also not near-term cures. Liver transplantation can replace a failing liver and can relieve liver-driven cholestasis, but it does not correct the inherited variant in the rest of the body or treat extrahepatic manifestations such as heart, kidney, vascular, skeletal, or eye involvement [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/). Future cell-based strategies would need to create or restore functional bile ducts, connect them properly to the biliary tree, engraft durably, and avoid immune and cancer risks, all while recognizing that Alagille Syndrome is a multisystem developmental condition [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/).

Small-molecule or RNA approaches that modulate the Notch pathway remain scientifically attractive but unproven for cure. In principle, a therapy that safely increased functional JAG1/NOTCH2 signaling could address the upstream mechanism, but Notch signaling is dose-, tissue-, and timing-sensitive, so too little signaling causes disease and too much or mistimed signaling could be harmful [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/). For that reason, recent successful therapies have focused on the downstream bile-acid problem rather than attempting systemic Notch activation [FDA Livmarli approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-treat-itching-children-rare-liver-disease) [FDA odevixibat approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-odevixibat-cholestatic-pruritus-patients-alagille-syndrome).

## Clinical Trials and Experimental Approaches

Maralixibat’s central Alagille program includes ICONIC, a multicenter study registered on ClinicalTrials.gov that evaluated maralixibat for cholestatic pruritus in Alagille Syndrome [ICONIC trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02160782). The peer-reviewed ICONIC report and FDA approval announcement support maralixibat’s benefit for itch reduction, while also emphasizing the need to monitor gastrointestinal adverse effects and liver tests during treatment [ICONIC maralixibat publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34748612/) [FDA Livmarli approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-treat-itching-children-rare-liver-disease).

Odevixibat’s Alagille program includes ASSERT, a phase 3 randomized clinical trial registered for children and adolescents with Alagille Syndrome [ASSERT trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04674761). FDA’s 2023 approval page states that odevixibat was approved for cholestatic pruritus in Alagille Syndrome, and EMA’s Bylvay page provides the European public regulatory assessment for the medicine [FDA odevixibat approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-odevixibat-cholestatic-pruritus-patients-alagille-syndrome) [EMA Bylvay EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/bylvay).

The broader registered trial landscape for Alagille Syndrome remains dominated by symptom-directed drug studies, follow-up studies, and observational work rather than first-in-human curative genetic correction [ClinicalTrials.gov Alagille studies](https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?cond=Alagille%20Syndrome). For families, this means that trial participation today is more likely to improve symptom control or knowledge of disease course than to offer a one-time cure [ClinicalTrials.gov Alagille studies](https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?cond=Alagille%20Syndrome).

## Methodologies and Scientific Approaches

Clinical studies use caregiver- or patient-reported itch scales, sleep and quality-of-life measures, serum bile acids, bilirubin and other liver tests, growth measures, xanthomas, adverse-event monitoring, and longer-term outcomes such as transplant-free survival [ICONIC trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT02160782) [ASSERT trial record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04674761). These endpoints matter because Alagille Syndrome can cause severe suffering from pruritus even before end-stage liver failure develops, and severe cholestatic symptoms can influence transplant decisions [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/).

Curative research is guided by the genetics and developmental biology of JAG1/NOTCH2 haploinsufficiency, meaning disease from having only one working gene copy [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/). Useful preclinical platforms must model bile-duct paucity and extrahepatic disease, while future delivery systems would need to reach the right liver and biliary cells and avoid unsafe Notch effects in the heart, vasculature, kidney, and other tissues [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/).

## Strengths, Limitations, and Challenges

The major strength of the recent field is that Alagille Syndrome now has mechanism-based oral therapies for cholestatic pruritus, replacing an era in which families often depended on older off-label antipruritics, surgical bile-diversion procedures, or transplant consideration for severe itch [FDA Livmarli approval](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-drug-treat-itching-children-rare-liver-disease) [FDA odevixibat approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-odevixibat-cholestatic-pruritus-patients-alagille-syndrome). The main limitation is that IBAT inhibitors do not repair bile ducts, correct JAG1 or NOTCH2 variants, or treat the syndrome’s non-liver manifestations, and some patients may have inadequate response or adverse effects [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/) [EMA Livmarli EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/livmarli) [EMA Bylvay EPAR](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/bylvay).

The biggest challenge for a true cure is that Alagille Syndrome is a developmental, multisystem, dosage-sensitive Notch-pathway disorder rather than a single-organ enzyme deficiency [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/). A future curative therapy would need to be early enough, targeted enough, and safe enough to improve bile-duct development or maintenance without creating harmful Notch signaling in other organs [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/).

## Outlook and Future Directions

A complete cure for Alagille Syndrome is not close in the near term, but recent progress has meaningfully changed symptom management and created a stronger clinical-trial framework for future disease-modifying therapies [ICONIC maralixibat publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34748612/) [FDA odevixibat approval](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-approves-odevixibat-cholestatic-pruritus-patients-alagille-syndrome). The milestones to watch are long-term real-world outcomes for IBAT inhibitors, better biomarkers predicting transplant risk, clearer genotype-phenotype links, and convincing preclinical evidence that JAG1/NOTCH2 correction or safe Notch modulation can preserve or rebuild bile ducts without harming other organs [ClinicalTrials.gov Alagille studies](https://clinicaltrials.gov/search?cond=Alagille%20Syndrome) [GeneReviews](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK1273/).
